
# Your First Afternoon at Prisms

*An orientation for incoming students. Margin notes have been retained where removal would damage the paper.*

Welcome to **Pengorgon's Remedial Institute for Summer Magical Studies**. You may call us **Prisms**. You're here because of an incident, an unfortunate exam result, or a building nobody can live in anymore. This summer, we'd like you to do a little better.

Professor Quill supervises afternoon study from the chair beside the window. His apparent sleeping is officially described as reflective observation. You are expected to remain at your desk.

> [!quote] Note in the margin
> If someone passes you a borrowed key and a note, take your pouch. An urgent errand beats another worksheet. Try to return before Quill wakes.

## Complete your admission record

Choose a **specialty school of magic** from [[Schools of Magic|the summer curriculum]]. This is your strongest subject. You can still try spells from any school.

Record **one background knack** and a connection that still needs your attention: "I know my way around the kitchens, and the cook needs her stolen recipe book back." Your knack means you can handle familiar, safe work without rolling. It doesn't add dice. Your people, promises, and unfinished problems may follow you into an adventure.

Begin at **Level 1 with 10 Composure**. Collect **twelve colored D6s**: four of your school's favored color, four of a different color you choose, and four randomly allocated using the Supply Office table. No color may occupy more than half your pouch; unacceptable random allocations are redrawn. Keep everything in **one opaque pouch**. See [[Students and Advancement|Admissions and Progress]] for the supply arrangements.

## Know what you are studying

Each die stands for an ingredient supply. The color tells you what kind of mana it carries:

| Color  | Mana family        | A familiar ingredient |
| ------ | ------------------ | --------------------- |
| Red    | Fire / Destruction | Brimstone             |
| Blue   | Water / Binding    | Vial of rainwater    |
| Green  | Life / Growth      | Mugwort extract       |
| Brown  | Earth / Shaping    | Clay                  |
| White  | Air / Warding      | Grain of salt          |
| Purple | Mind / Illusion    | Dried mandrake root   |
| Black  | Death / Spirit     | Rowan-wood flakes     |

**Your school is how you cast the spell. Mana is what it uses and what it becomes.** An evocator may strike with flame, water, roots, or flying books. Consult [[Dice and Mana|The Component Cupboard]]. Please use dice at the table; actual brimstone is not required.

## Before attempting anything impressive

Declare your **intended effect, target, school, and mana color**. Agree the difficulty with the **Professor** before drawing. The **Professor** is the player running the game.

Draw blindly and roll **five dice**, or **seven at Level 1 when the technique belongs to your specialty**. Use your full allowance. Do not select ingredients, return unwanted colors, or reroll disappointing faces.

| Exercise    | Required matching faces | Typical undertaking                                           |
| ----------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Routine     | Any pair                | One nearby target or a small obstacle                         |
| Demanding   | Three alike             | A substantial obstacle, a room, or up to three targets        |
| Exceptional | Four alike              | A major magical obstacle, a large area, or up to five targets |

Look at your **largest matching group**. Two 1s are as good as two 6s; two separate pairs do not make a triple. Meet the requirement and the magic takes hold. Fall short and it **backfires**. Your intention and difficulty remain fixed after the draw.

## Inspect what you actually used

Count **every drawn color**, including dice outside the matching group. A color leads if it outnumbers each other color; it need not exceed half the handful.

- **The faces succeed and your intended color leads alone:** your intended spell works as you expected.
- **The faces succeed and another color leads alone:** a useful spell works through that mana instead. Fire Sword may become **Fires Word**, pelting the target with romance novels.
- **The faces succeed and colors tie for the lead:** **Wild magic** supplies an unintended useful effect and a complication. A Red/Green Fire Sword might become a flaming rosebush that menaces your pursuer while blocking your route.
- **The faces fall short:** your cast backfires. The leading color themes the mishap; tied leaders combine their themes into one backfire.

**You normally choose your unexpected effect or backfire.** For successful Wild magic, choose both the useful effect and its complication. Follow the colors and the permitted scope, strength, or severity. If you need help, classmates suggest ideas and the Professor chooses one. If something already in the scene offers an obvious way for the spell to go wrong and still help, the Professor may use it.

If the cast succeeds, you get something useful. Wild magic brings trouble as well, but it still helps. A different color winning doesn't automatically add a penalty. A failed cast remains a backfire however clever the explanation. See [[Spellcasting|Approved Casting Procedure]].

**Return every die and mix the pouch after every cast.** You used a pinch of the ingredient. Success and failure both leave the supply available.

## How much magic is enough?

Successful spells have **2 points of strength at Routine difficulty, 4 at Demanding, and 6 at Exceptional**. An attack deals that much damage, healing restores that much Composure, and a shield absorbs that much damage. If the largest matching group exceeds the requirement, add **one point**, once. Never add the rolled faces together.

Choose one main effect per cast. Divide points between multiple targets before drawing; four points are four altogether, not four each. Utility magic follows the agreed scope. Duration and particular applications appear in [[Spell Effects and Backfires|Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps]].

For backfires, **one die short** means a brief complication or **1 damage**; **two short**, a scene-lasting complication or **2 damage**; **three short**, a major scene problem or **3 damage**. Choose one consequence: damage or a complication. Do not automatically apply both. Missing eyebrows may accompany either at no additional charge.

## When everyone starts shouting

Describe where you are: beside the valve, behind the hedge, or across the greenhouse. Say what you can reach and what stands in your way; you do not need measured distances.

When orderly turns are needed, agree who acts first. Each student gets **one meaningful action**, ordinary movement, and brief speech. After everyone acts, the scene's announced trouble takes its action; a major finale may have two announced actions. The Professor describes evident danger before your turns so you can respond.

Spend your action helping a named classmate with a particular task and their next relevant draw gains **one extra die from their own pouch**. Helpers do not roll separately, bonuses do not stack, and help expires at the end of the next student phase.

Use the same casting rules when you try to sneak, climb, persuade someone, or investigate something risky. Explain your quiet-foot charm, summoned handhold, or other technique. Even avoiding deliberate magic may bring out a reflex enchantment. Safe ordinary actions need no draw: you may walk through an open door without enchanting it.

## Your eyebrows and your whereabouts

Damage reduces your **10 Composure**. At zero you are **Dazed**; classmates have a chance to help before Nurse Bell's wards collect you. Collection brings recovery and inconvenient questions, not removal from the adventure. A safe breather after a pressured scene restores **2 Composure once**; proper rest restores all ten. Consult [[Turns Trouble and Recovery|Conduct During an Incident]].

Your cohort shares **Faculty Suspicion, from 0 to 6**. Clear evidence raises it: a loud disturbance, an infirmary collection, or a conspicuous mess in a monitored area. A backfire does not raise it automatically. Staff grow curious, investigate, and eventually intervene. Being caught earns a fitting consequence, not the loss of your level or permanent supplies. See [[Threats and Faculty|Faculty Attention]].

> [!quote] Advice from an older student
> "We were helping" is a good start. It's rarely a good excuse.

## Specimens and corrective supplies

Helping, exploring, and solving problems can earn a usable ingredient: one die per significant scene, up to three per student per expedition. The Professor names its color before you accept it. Being caught may also bring two compulsory random unwanted dice, up to four corrective dice per expedition. Record additions, mix them into your one pouch, and keep the half-pouch limit. Your draw stays the same. Return these temporary additions at debrief; your normal advancement rewards remain. See [[Students and Advancement]].

## What you bring home

Afternoon study includes compulsory unfamiliar supplies: **six extra dice at Level 1**, mixed into your existing pouch for eighteen altogether. Take the packet when you slip out. Older students receive larger packets; unwanted ingredients are officially character-building.

Four major adventures can advance your cohort through **five levels**. At each advancement debrief, return the loaned packet and gain **two permanent dice in colors you choose**, respecting the half-pouch limit. Your specialty draw grows to eight at Level 3 and nine at Level 5; your ordinary draw stays five. At Level 5, return the whole packet; further debriefs may permit recoloring existing supplies instead of adding more.

Unauthorized expeditions can earn practical credit once you explain what you learned. You still need a hall pass to leave class. You become better prepared, but Prisms keeps finding unfamiliar things for your pouch.

Begin with [[Please Water the Greenhouse|Please Water the Greenhouse]]. Read the student instructions; the sealed appendix belongs to the Professor. There is a note under your worksheet, a borrowed key, and a noise Quill apparently cannot hear.
