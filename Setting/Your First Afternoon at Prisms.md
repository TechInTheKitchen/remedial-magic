
# Your First Afternoon at Prisms

*An orientation for incoming students. Margin notes have been retained where removal would damage the paper.*

Welcome to **Pengorgon's Remedial Institute for Summer Magical Studies**. You may call us **Prisms**. Your admission follows an incident, an unfortunate examination result, or a building that can no longer be inhabited. This summer is your opportunity to demonstrate improvement.

Professor Quill supervises afternoon study from the chair beside the window. His apparent sleeping is officially described as reflective observation. You are expected to remain at your desk.

> [!quote] Note in the margin
> If someone passes you a borrowed key and a note don't question it, take the excuse to leave and run.

## Complete your admission record

Choose a **specialty school of magic** from [[Schools of Magic|the summer curriculum]]. This is the subject you are particularly good at, not the only subject you might know.

Record **one background knack** and a connection that still needs your attention: "I know my way around the kitchens, and the cook needs her stolen recipe book back." Your knack establishes ordinary competence in familiar, safe work; it grants no extra dice. Your people, promises, and unfinished problems may follow you into an adventure.

Begin at **Level 1 with 10 Composure**. Collect **twelve colored D6s**: four of your school's favored color, four of a different color you choose, and four randomly allocated by another student. No color may occupy more than half your pouch; unacceptable random allocations are redrawn. Keep everything in **one opaque pouch**. See [[Students and Advancement|Admissions and Progress]] for the supply arrangements.

## Know what you are studying 

Each die represents a supply of an ingredient. Its color identifies its mana:

| Color  | Mana family        | A familiar ingredient |
| ------ | ------------------ | --------------------- |
| Red    | Fire / Destruction | Brimstone             |
| Blue   | Water / Binding    | Vial of Rain Water    |
| Green  | Life / Growth      | Mugwort Extract       |
| Brown  | Earth / Shaping    | Clay                  |
| White  | Air / Warding      | Salt - Table          |
| Purple | Mind / Illusion    | Dried Mandrake Root   |
| Black  | Death / Spirit     | Rowan Wood Flakes     |

**School describes your technique; mana describes its substance and expression.** An evocator may strike with flame, water, roots, or solid words. Consult [[Dice and Mana|The Component Cupboard]]. Please use dice at the table; actual brimstone is not required.

## Before attempting anything impressive

Declare your **intended effect, target, school, and mana color**. Agree the difficulty with the **Professor** before drawing. Here, "Professor" means the person overseeing the tale at your table. You need not wake Quill to obtain a ruling.

Draw blindly and roll **five dice**, or **seven at Level 1 when the technique belongs to your specialty**. Use your full allowance. Do not select ingredients, return unwanted colors, or reroll disappointing faces.

| Exercise    | Required matching faces | Typical undertaking                                           |
| ----------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Routine     | Any pair                | One nearby target or a small obstacle                         |
| Demanding   | Three alike             | A substantial obstacle, a room, or up to three targets        |
| Exceptional | Four alike              | A major magical obstacle, a large area, or up to five targets |

Look at your **largest matching group**. Two 1s are as good as two 6s; two separate pairs do not make a triple. Meet the requirement and the magic takes hold. Fall short and it **backfires**. Your intention and difficulty remain fixed after the draw.

## Inspect what you actually used

Count **every drawn color**, including dice outside the matching group. A color and therefor mana leads if it outnumbers each other color; it need not exceed half the handful.

- **The faces succeed and your intended color leads alone:** your intended spell works as you expected.
- **The faces succeed and another color leads alone:** a useful spell works through that mana instead. Fire Sword may become **Fires Word**, striking with solid language.
- **The faces succeed and colors tie for the lead:** **Wild magic** supplies an unintended useful effect and a complication. A Red/Green Fire Sword might become a flaming rosebush that menaces your pursuer while blocking your route.
- **The faces fall short:** your cast backfires. The leading color themes the mishap; tied leaders combine their themes into one backfire.

**You normally choose your unexpected effect or backfire.** For successful Wild magic, choose both the useful effect and its complication. Follow the colors and the permitted scope, strength, or severity. If you need help, classmates suggest ideas and the Professor chooses one. An obvious opportunity already present in the scene may also let the Professor explain how success arrives by an unfortunate route.

A successful substitution remains useful. A Wild complication does not cancel its benefit; a wrong-color success has no compulsory extra penalty. A failed cast remains a backfire however clever the explanation. See [[Spellcasting|Approved Casting Procedure]].

**Return every die and mix the pouch after every cast.** You used a pinch of the ingredient. Success and failure both leave the supply available.

## How much magic is enough?

Successful spells have **2 points of strength at Routine difficulty, 4 at Demanding, and 6 at Exceptional**. An attack deals that much damage, healing restores that much Composure, and a shield absorbs that much damage. If the largest matching group exceeds the requirement, add **one point**, once. Never add the rolled faces together.

Choose one principal effect per cast. Divide points between multiple targets before drawing; four points are four altogether, not four each. Utility magic follows the agreed scope. Duration and particular applications appear in [[Spell Effects and Backfires|Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps]].

For backfires, **one die short** means a brief complication or **1 damage**; **two short**, a scene-lasting complication or **2 damage**; **three short**, a major scene problem or **3 damage**. Record one principal consequence, not damage and a complication automatically. Missing eyebrows may accompany either at no additional charge.

## When everyone starts shouting

Describe where you are: beside the valve, behind the hedge, or across the greenhouse. Measuring the corridor will not save your favorite shirt or beloved eyebrows.

When orderly turns are needed, agree who acts first. Each student gets **one meaningful action**, ordinary movement, and brief speech. After everyone acts, the scene's announced trouble takes its action; a major finale may have two announced actions. The Professor describes evident danger before your turns so you can respond.

Spend your action helping a named classmate with a particular task and their next relevant draw gains **one extra die from their own pouch**. Helpers do not roll separately, bonuses do not stack, and help expires at the end of the next student phase.

Risky sneaking, climbing, persuasion, and investigation use the same casting procedure. Explain your quiet-foot charm, summoned handhold, or other technique. Even avoiding deliberate magic may bring out a reflex enchantment. Safe ordinary actions need no draw: you may walk through an open door without enchanting it.

## Your eyebrows and your whereabouts

Damage reduces your **10 Composure**. At zero you are **Dazed**; classmates have a chance to help before Nurse Bell's wards collect you. Collection brings recovery and inconvenient questions, not removal from the adventure. A safe breather after a pressured scene restores **2 Composure once**; proper rest restores all ten. Consult [[Turns Trouble and Recovery|Conduct During an Incident]].

Your cohort shares **Faculty Suspicion, from 0 to 6**. Clear evidence raises it: a loud disturbance, an infirmary collection, or a conspicuous mess in a monitored area. A backfire does not raise it automatically. Staff grow curious, investigate, and eventually intervene. Being caught earns a fitting consequence, not the loss of your level or permanent supplies. See [[Threats and Faculty|Faculty Attention]].

> [!quote] Advice from an older student
> "We were helping" is a good beginning. It is rarely a complete explanation.

## What you bring home

Afternoon study includes compulsory unfamiliar supplies: **six extra dice at Level 1**, mixed into your existing pouch for eighteen altogether. Take the packet when you slip out. Older students receive larger packets; unwanted ingredients are officially character-building.

Four major adventures can advance your cohort through **five levels**. At each advancement debrief, return the loaned packet and gain **two permanent dice in colors you choose**, respecting the half-pouch limit. Your specialty draw grows to eight at Level 3 and nine at Level 5; your ordinary draw stays five. At Level 5, return the whole packet; further debriefs may permit recoloring existing supplies instead of adding more.

Unauthorized expeditions can earn practical credit once you explain what you learned. Permission to leave class is a separate matter. You become better prepared, but Prisms keeps finding unfamiliar things for your pouch.

Begin with [[Please Water the Greenhouse|Please Water the Greenhouse]]. Read the student instructions; the sealed appendix belongs to the Professor. There is a note under your worksheet, a borrowed key, and a noise Quill apparently cannot hear.
