
# Troublesome Specimens and Faculty Attention

Consult: [[Turns Trouble and Recovery]] | [[Spell Effects and Backfires]] | [[Please Water the Greenhouse]]

> [!quote] Groundskeeper's advice
> If it has teeth, ask whether it is supposed to. If it answers, fetch a teacher.

## Recognizing troublesome specimens

The Professor uses the records below for troublesome specimens. They do not need a dice pouch. The Professor tells you what they're about to do before your turns. If you don't stop them, they act in the threat phase. You can get out of the way, change their plan, contain them, or brace yourself. A broom waving its handle is usually providing sufficient notice.

| Threat size | Composure or Stability | Damage per threat action | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small nuisance | 2 | 1 | Animated broom, biting flowerpot, mischievous ink blot |
| Scene trouble | 6 | 2 | Territorial topiary, overenthusiastic practice dummy |
| Major problem | 10 | 3 | Runaway school enchantment, enormous greenhouse root |

Use Composure for people and creatures; use Stability for unstable spells and animated objects. At zero, a threat is exhausted, contained, dispersed, or otherwise out of the scene. Damage need not imply injury or death.

These totals assume **three students**. For each student above or below three, adjust a scene threat's Stability by 2 and a major problem's Stability by 3, with minimums of 2 and 4 respectively. Small nuisances stay at 2. Set the total at the start of the encounter. Keep it there even if a student becomes Dazed or leaves. Damage and the number of threat actions stay the same.

For example, a four-student major problem has 13 Stability; a two-student one has 7. Larger groups face sturdier threats, with the same damage and number of actions.

For each threat, the Professor notes four things:

- **Want:** what it is trying to accomplish.
- **Warning:** what students notice before its next action.
- **Action:** one damage effect or one concrete complication.
- **Way out:** a non-attack solution, such as appeasing, disconnecting, distracting, or containing it.

A suitable successful spell can settle the problem without wearing down all its Stability. Set the difficulty for the solution you're trying. An inventive physical solution is also acceptable. Risky handling follows the same magical task-draw procedure.

Do not add separate defense rolls to successful attacks. Match the spell difficulty to the declared scope and any established obstacle. A tough target usually has more Stability. It doesn't automatically make the spell harder to cast.

### Responding to announced trouble

Use **one threat action for the whole scene by default**, even if several nuisance creatures are present. A group can act together as one threat. For a major concluding incident, the Professor may announce up to two distinct threat actions before student turns. Each action deals damage to one target or creates one complication; additional targets split its damage budget.

If the students stop the announced action, it does not happen. The threat can change its plan for the next round. Environmental hazards use the same threat actions. They don't deal extra damage on the side.

An area hazard gives students an opportunity to move, protect someone, or disable its cause before consequences land. Describe blocked routes and dangerous positions rather than measuring distance.

If an announced damaging action still reaches its target after the students act, apply its damage without another roll. State whether the threat can pursue someone who moves away; moving away only avoids the attack if the student actually reaches safety. If the announced action is prevented, do not replace it with an unannounced attack that phase.

A Routine distraction can buy one threat phase. Removing a threat for the whole scene uses the solution's agreed scope and difficulty; changing the description to "a very distracting noise" does not make a major problem a Routine task. Use the restraint guidance in [[Spell Effects and Backfires]].

## Faculty attention

Use a shared **Suspicion track from 0 to 6** for a quest. It tracks the risk of being found out.

Increase Suspicion by one when a distinct incident leaves clear evidence for staff: an infirmary collection, a loud unauthorized disturbance, or a conspicuous mess in a monitored area. The same incident adds only one even if several students are collected or several windows break. A backfire in an isolated area does not automatically raise Suspicion.

| Suspicion | Faculty response                                                                             |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–1       | Quill dozes; staff assume the students are still in class.                                   |
| 2–3       | A teacher checks in or a prefect starts asking questions.                                    |
| 4–5       | Staff investigate directly; students must explain, hide evidence, or work under observation. |
| 6         | A teacher intervenes and imposes one consequence. Reset Suspicion to 3 afterward.            |

Use each response when Suspicion first reaches that band, rather than every round. If it falls and rises again, a new incident may bring another response. A teacher showing up doesn't mean a fight has started.

Taking time to repair the damage or give staff an honest, useful account may lower Suspicion by one. Repeating the same excuse earns no further reduction. Risky concealment or persuasion uses a bag draw. A wrong-color success provides a useful alternative; Wild magic adds a complication. Neither has to make your original cover story convincing.

Suspicion returns to zero after the quest's debrief. Actual consequences may last through the next assignment.

## Corrective arrangements

Choose one consequence suited to the incident:

- **Supervised practical:** a teacher accompanies the next scene and asks inconvenient questions.
- **Repair duty:** restore the thing the cohort damaged before the debrief.
- **Corrective homework:** demonstrate the failed technique safely in a short follow-up scene.
- **Restricted access:** a convenient cupboard or route is unavailable until the students earn permission.
- **Corrective supplies:** each student implicated in the incident receives two compulsory random ingredient dice outside their declared preferred colors. Follow [[Students and Advancement#Corrective supply packets]]; after four corrective dice per student in an assignment, choose another consequence.

Issue corrective supplies when a teacher catches the students or works out who was involved. Raising Suspicion on its own isn't enough. At Suspicion 6, this can be the one required consequence; do not automatically add it to another punishment. Issue the dice before the next cast, never during a draw already underway.
Detention may mean repairs, homework, or a teacher looking over your shoulder. You keep your level and permanent supplies, and you still take part in the adventure. Finish with the normal debrief and rewards even if staff had to step in.
