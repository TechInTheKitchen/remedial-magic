
# Troublesome Specimens and Faculty Attention

Consult: [[Turns Trouble and Recovery]] | [[Spell Effects and Backfires]] | [[Please Water the Greenhouse]]

> [!quote] Groundskeeper's advice
> If it has teeth, ask whether it is supposed to. If it answers, fetch a teacher.

## Recognizing troublesome specimens

Your Professor records troublesome specimens without drawing a separate pouch for them. Their intentions are described before your turns; unresolved actions follow in the threat phase. You may prevent, redirect, contain, or withstand them. A broom waving its handle is usually providing sufficient notice.

| Threat size | Composure or Stability | Damage per threat action | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small nuisance | 2 | 1 | Animated broom, biting flowerpot, mischievous ink blot |
| Scene trouble | 6 | 2 | Territorial topiary, overenthusiastic practice dummy |
| Major problem | 10 | 3 | Runaway school enchantment, enormous greenhouse root |

Use Composure for people and creatures; use Stability for unstable spells and animated objects. At zero, a threat is exhausted, contained, dispersed, or otherwise out of the scene. Damage need not imply injury or death.

These totals assume **three students**. For each student above or below three, adjust a scene threat's Stability by 2 and a major problem's Stability by 3, with minimums of 2 and 4 respectively. Small nuisances stay at 2. Set the total when the encounter begins and do not change it mid-scene because students are Dazed or elsewhere. Damage and the shared threat-action budget stay unchanged.

For example, a four-student major problem has 13 Stability; a two-student one has 7. The register allows for the size of your cohort without authorizing harsher consequences.

Professors complete four entries for each troublesome specimen:

- **Want:** what it is trying to accomplish.
- **Warning:** what students notice before its next action.
- **Action:** one damage effect or one concrete complication.
- **Way out:** a non-attack solution, such as appeasing, disconnecting, distracting, or containing it.

You may resolve a specimen by a suitable successful effect whose scope fits the difficulty; exhausting its Stability is not compulsory. An inventive physical solution is also acceptable. Risky handling follows the same magical task-draw procedure.

Do not add separate defense rolls to successful attacks. Match the spell difficulty to the declared scope and any established obstacle. Toughness is normally represented by Stability, not an automatic demand for harder matches.

### Responding to announced trouble

Use **one threat action for the whole scene by default**, even if several nuisance creatures are present. A group can act together as one threat. For a major concluding incident, the Professor may announce up to two distinct threat actions before student turns. Each action deals damage to one target or creates one complication; additional targets split its damage budget.

If the students stop the announced action, it does not happen. The threat can change its plan for the next round. An unresolved environmental hazard uses this same action budget rather than dealing extra automatic damage on top of everything else.

An area hazard gives students an opportunity to move, protect someone, or disable its cause before consequences land. Describe blocked routes and dangerous positions rather than measuring distance.

If an announced damaging action still reaches its target after the students act, apply its damage without another roll. State whether the threat can pursue someone who moves away; a free reposition only avoids an attack when it actually puts the student somewhere safe. If the announced action is prevented, do not replace it with an unannounced attack that phase.

A Routine distraction can buy one threat phase. Removing a threat for the whole scene uses the solution's agreed scope and difficulty; changing the description to "a very distracting noise" does not make a major problem a Routine task. Use the restraint guidance in [[Spell Effects and Backfires]].

## The faculty attention register

Use a shared **Suspicion track from 0 to 6** for a quest. It tracks the risk of being found out.

Increase Suspicion by one when a distinct incident leaves clear evidence for staff: an infirmary collection, a loud unauthorized disturbance, or a conspicuous mess in a monitored area. The same incident adds only one even if several students are collected or several windows break. A backfire in an isolated area does not automatically raise Suspicion.

| Suspicion | Faculty response                                                                             |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–1       | Quill dozes; staff assume the students are still in class.                                   |
| 2–3       | A teacher checks in or a prefect starts asking questions.                                    |
| 4–5       | Staff investigate directly; students must explain, hide evidence, or work under observation. |
| 6         | A teacher intervenes and imposes one consequence. Reset Suspicion to 3 afterward.            |

Resolve a response when the track first enters its band, not every round. Falling and re-entering a band can prompt a new response if a new incident warrants it. Faculty arrival is not automatically a combat encounter.

A meaningful scene spent repairing damage or giving an honest, useful explanation may lower Suspicion by one. Repeating the same excuse earns no further reduction. The register remembers it even if the teacher does not. Risky concealment or persuasion uses a bag draw; a wrong-color or Wild success may create a new complication rather than guaranteeing the cover story.

Suspicion returns to zero after the quest's debrief. Actual consequences may last through the next assignment.

## Corrective arrangements

Choose one consequence suited to the incident:

- **Supervised practical:** a teacher accompanies the next scene and asks inconvenient questions.
- **Repair duty:** restore the thing the cohort damaged before the debrief.
- **Corrective homework:** demonstrate the failed technique safely in a short follow-up scene.
- **Restricted access:** a convenient cupboard or route is unavailable until the students earn permission.
- **Public explanation:** present what happened to classmates, including the part involving the shrub.

Corrective work supplements your education. It does not revoke your level, confiscate your permanent allowance, or exclude you from practical participation. An assignment completed with faculty intervention still receives its normal debrief and rewards.
