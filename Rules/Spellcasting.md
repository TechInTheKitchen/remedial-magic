
# Approved Casting Procedure

Consult: [[Your First Afternoon at Prisms]] | [[Dice and Mana]] | [[Schools of Magic]] | [[Students and Advancement]]

> [!quote] Practical Examination Instructions
> Successfully casting a spell and casting the requested spell are marked separately.

## Before opening your pouch

Tell the Professor what you want the spell to do, who or what it affects, and which **school and mana color** you plan to use. Agree on the difficulty before you draw. The technique has to fit the school: calling a falling brick "an exercise in Divination" won't get you a bigger handful.

Once you draw, stick with the intention and difficulty you agreed on.

## The seven steps

1. **Declare your intention.** Say what you want to do, which school you are using, and which mana color you intend.
2. **Confirm difficulty.** Routine requires two alike, Demanding three, Exceptional four.
3. **Draw blindly.** Draw five dice, or your specialty allowance for your level. Add one die if a classmate is helping. Keep each die out until the cast is over. If you need more dice than the pouch holds, draw the whole pouch once.
4. **Roll and inspect the largest matching group.** Any face value qualifies. Meet or exceed the required difficulty to succeed.
5. **Count every drawn color.** Count the dice that matched and those that didn't. The most common color shapes the spell. A tie for most common means Wild magic.
6. **Resolve the result.** Consult the outcome table below and [[Spell Effects and Backfires]].
7. **Return every die and mix the pouch.** Each cast uses only a pinch of the ingredients. Keep your supplies whether the spell works or backfires.

Take your full allowance without looking or feeling for particular dice. Keep what you draw and roll it once. You can only change the draw when a rule says so; a harder spell doesn't earn extra dice.

## What constitutes a match

| Difficulty | Required group | Qualifying example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | At least 2 alike | Two 1s, two 6s, or any other pair |
| Demanding | At least 3 alike | Three 2s or any other triple |
| Exceptional | At least 4 alike | Four 5s or any other group of four |

A pair of 1s is as good as a pair of 6s. **Two pairs do not make a triple.** A larger group also passes an easier difficulty and may earn the strength bonus in [[Spell Effects and Backfires]].

A smaller group produces a backfire even if it would have passed an easier exercise.

> [!quote] Student annotation
> Seven dice always contain a pair. The trick is persuading the mana to be the spell you wanted.

## Recording the outcome

| Numerical result | Color result                        | Outcome                                                                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meets difficulty | Intended color leads alone          | Your intended spell succeeds.                                          |
| Meets difficulty | Another color leads alone           | Your spell succeeds through an unexpected expression of that mana.     |
| Meets difficulty | Two or more colors tie for the lead | Wild magic: an unintended spell takes hold.                            |
| Below difficulty | Any color result                    | Your cast backfires; its colors supply the mishap's style and outcome. |

The leading color just needs more dice than each other color. It doesn't need more than half the handful. Three Red, two Blue, and two Green resolve as Red. Two Red, two Blue, and one Green are Wild. A tie below the leader does not count.

### Unanticipated substitution

When a different color wins, try to find another way for the spell to do the job. Purple may turn Fire Sword into **Fires Word**. A Green shield may become a hedge; a Blue shield may become ice. If the new mana can't do what you wanted, the successful cast still gives you something useful.

### Wild results

When colors tie, use them together to imagine what happens. You can't pick your favorite and count it as the intended spell. There is no Wild ingredient to buy.

**The caster chooses what the Wild spell does and what trouble comes with it.** Use the tied colors and what's happening around you. It doesn't have to do the job you planned, but the Professor checks that it stays within the agreed scope and strength.

If everyone is stuck, let the spell do the job in an unexpected way and add one complication from the tied colors. Successful Wild complications do not automatically damage the caster. A failed Wild cast receives **one normal backfire**, not an additional successful spell or one backfire per color.

A Red/Green tie on Fire Sword might produce a flaming rosebush: useful against pursuers, inconvenient for your intended route, and difficult to explain to Groundskeeper Moss.

## Explaining an unintended result

You normally choose how your own spell goes sideways, whether it's a useful substitution, Wild magic, or a backfire. Use the leading mana and stay within the result's scope, strength, or severity. You don't have to come up with a pun.

Where the scene already offers an obvious way to succeed by going wrong, the Professor may follow that opening directly. A misplaced gust might knock the sought-after key off its hook and into the soup. That twist has to come from something already in the scene, and a successful roll still gives you a useful result. If the faces fell short, it's a backfire, even if the accident happens to help.

**Ask your classmates if you get stuck.** They can suggest ideas until the Professor chooses one. The Professor also checks your own suggestion against the rules; work out any changes together. There's no extra roll. Use the incident ledger for ideas when you need it.

A wrong-color success gives you something useful without an automatic penalty. A successful Wild spell needs both a useful benefit and a concrete complication. A backfire needs the consequence specified by its shortfall. Keep the result you rolled as you describe what happened.
## An example from the marking ledger

A Level 1 Evocation specialist attempts a Routine Fire Sword and draws seven dice.

- **Faces:** 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6. A pair meets Routine difficulty. The two pairs do not form a triple.
- **Colors:** one Red, six Purple. Purple leads alone.
- **Result:** Fires Word: the target is pelted with romance novels.

The same faces fail a Demanding attempt, falling short by one. A Minor Purple backfire might send a book flying back at the student.

## Keeping a useful allowance

What you put in the pouch affects what comes out. More of a color makes it more likely to lead; a spread of colors brings more variety and more chances of a tie. New supplies can make your favorite color less common, even though the pouch is fuller.

Observe the Balanced Reagent Policy: no color exceeds half the pouch. Mix the six-to-fourteen compulsory practical dice into that **same pouch**. Record the loaned colors on paper and draw everything from the same pouch. See [[Students and Advancement]].
