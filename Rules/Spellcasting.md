
# Approved Casting Procedure

Consult: [[Your First Afternoon at Prisms]] | [[Dice and Mana]] | [[Schools of Magic]] | [[Students and Advancement]]

> [!quote] Practical Examination Instructions
> Successfully casting a spell and casting the requested spell are marked separately.

## Before opening your pouch

Explain your intended **effect, target, school, and mana color** to your Professor. Agree on difficulty before drawing. Your technique must actually belong to the claimed department; calling a falling brick "an exercise in Divination" will not improve your allowance.

Once you draw, the intention and difficulty are fixed for that attempt.

## The seven steps

1. **Declare your intention.** State what you mean to accomplish, using which school and intended mana.
2. **Confirm difficulty.** Routine requires two alike, Demanding three, Exceptional four.
3. **Draw blindly.** Draw five dice ordinarily, or the specialty draw authorized for your level. Add one helper die if granted. Draw without replacement within the cast; if the request exceeds your supplies, draw the whole pouch once.
4. **Roll and inspect the largest matching group.** Any face value qualifies. Meet or exceed the required group to succeed.
5. **Count every drawn color.** Matching and non-matching dice all contribute. A sole leading color determines expression; a tie for the lead produces Wild magic.
6. **Resolve the result.** Consult the outcome table below and [[Spell Effects and Backfires]].
7. **Return every die and mix the pouch.** You have used a pinch, not emptied a jar. Return supplies on success and failure alike.

Use your full authorized draw. Do not select dice by sight or touch, draw fewer voluntarily, redraw unwanted colors, or reroll faces. Only modifiers expressly granted by the regulations apply. Difficult work does not itself enlarge your draw.

## What constitutes a match

| Difficulty | Required group | Qualifying example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | At least 2 alike | Two 1s, two 6s, or any other pair |
| Demanding | At least 3 alike | Three 2s or any other triple |
| Exceptional | At least 4 alike | Four 5s or any other group of four |

A pair of 1s qualifies as readily as a pair of 6s. **Separate groups do not add together:** two pairs are not a triple. A larger group meets a smaller requirement. An oversized group grants the single strength bonus described in [[Spell Effects and Backfires]].

A smaller group produces a backfire even if it would have passed an easier exercise.

> [!quote] Student annotation
> Seven dice always contain a pair. The trick is persuading the pair to be involved in the spell you wanted.

## Recording the outcome

| Numerical result | Color result                        | Outcome                                                                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meets difficulty | Intended color leads alone          | Your intended spell succeeds.                                          |
| Meets difficulty | Another color leads alone           | Your spell succeeds through an unexpected expression of that mana.     |
| Meets difficulty | Two or more colors tie for the lead | Wild magic: an unintended spell takes hold.                            |
| Below difficulty | Any color result                    | Your cast backfires; its colors supply the mishap's style and outcome. |

A leader need only outnumber each other color. It need not exceed half the draw. Three Red, two Blue, and two Green resolve as Red. Two Red, two Blue, and one Green are Wild. A tie below the leader does not count.

### Unanticipated substitution

Where possible, an unexpected color retains your purpose by another means. Purple may turn Fire Sword into **Fires Word**. A Green shield may become a hedge; a Blue shield may become ice. Numerical success supplies a practical benefit even when the original purpose cannot reasonably survive the substitution.

### Wild results

Wild is an outcome, not an eighth ingredient. You may not select one tied color and claim the intended spell.

**The caster chooses the Wild spell's useful effect and complication**, inspired by the tied leading colors and immediate circumstances. It need not accomplish the original intention. The Professor checks that it fits the agreed scope and strength.

If everyone is out of ideas, retain the action's useful benefit and add one concrete inconvenience from the tied colors. Successful Wild complications do not automatically damage the caster. A failed Wild cast receives **one normal backfire**, not an additional successful spell or one backfire per color.

A Red/Green tie on Fire Sword might produce a flaming rosebush: useful against pursuers, inconvenient for your intended route, and difficult to explain to Groundskeeper Moss.

## Explaining an unintended result

**Your ingredients, your explanation.** The caster normally chooses what happens when a spell becomes an unexpected substitution, successful Wild spell, or backfire. Follow the leading mana and the result's permitted scope, strength, or severity. A pun is welcome; it is not an examination requirement.

Where the scene already offers an obvious way to succeed by going wrong, the Professor may follow that opening directly. A misplaced gust might knock the sought-after key off its hook and into the soup. This exception must grow from circumstances already established, and numerical success must still provide a useful benefit. A failed match remains a backfire, however convenient its incidental consequences may be.

**Ask the table when inspiration fails.** If you cannot find an explanation, or would like help, classmates offer ideas until the Professor chooses one that fits. The Professor also checks your own choice against the regulations; adjust it together if necessary. No extra roll is required. The incident ledger supplies inspiration rather than a compulsory lottery.

A wrong-color success needs a useful substitution, with no compulsory extra penalty. A successful Wild spell needs both a useful benefit and a concrete complication. A backfire needs the consequence specified by its shortfall. Your explanation changes what the magic looks like, not the marks already earned.

> [!quote] Student annotation
> You are responsible for explaining your own explosion. Assistance is available from everyone who heard it.

## An example from the marking ledger

A Level 1 Evocation specialist attempts a Routine Fire Sword and draws seven dice.

- **Faces:** 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6. A pair meets Routine difficulty. The two pairs do not form a triple.
- **Colors:** one Red, six Purple. Purple leads alone.
- **Result:** Fires Word, a successful attack with solid language.

The same faces fail a Demanding attempt, falling short by one. A Minor Purple backfire might send the words back at the student.

## Keeping a useful allowance

Your component choices influence which magic appears. Concentrating a permitted color improves reliability; distributing supplies increases variety and leading ties. Adding supplies can dilute a familiar color rather than improve it.

Observe the Balanced Reagent Policy: no color exceeds half the pouch. Mix the six-to-fourteen compulsory practical dice into that **same pouch**. Loan records are bookkeeping, not permission to make separate draws. See [[Students and Advancement]].
