
# Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps

Consult: [[Spellcasting]] | [[Dice and Mana]] | [[Schools of Magic]] | [[Students and Advancement]]

> [!quote] Practical Magic, Chapter One
> Decide what the spell should do before discovering what it has done.

Read this chapter before attempting to improve upon the syllabus. The approved scopes and strengths are listed below. [[Turns Trouble and Recovery|Conduct During an Incident]] covers the arrangements should improvement prove premature.

## Declare the exercise

Before drawing, declare your intended **effect**, **target**, **school**, and **mana color**. Your Professor confirms difficulty from scope. An impressive spell name does not count as an additional effect.

Declare one principal effect per cast. An attack may sparkle magnificently; it may not also heal you, restrain the target, and open a portal on the same mark. Separate substantial benefits require separate actions and casts. Decorative flourishes are free. Higher difficulty expands the one chosen effect.

## Authorized scope

| Difficulty | Required match | Typical scope | Effect strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | 2 alike | One nearby target, a small object, or one immediate obstacle | 2 points |
| Demanding | 3 alike | Up to three nearby targets, a room-sized effect, or a substantial obstacle | 4 points total |
| Exceptional | 4 alike | Up to five nearby targets, a large area, or a major magical obstacle | 6 points total |

The Exceptional designation does not authorize unlimited wishes or permanent resurrection. Such work requires special circumstances and permission from your Professor. Four matching dice are not a letter of recommendation.

**Nearby** means within the same immediate scene and clearly reachable by the described magic. Moving between adjacent positions or across a classroom is Routine; crossing a substantial gap or moving a group is Demanding. Judge reach from the actual obstacle; no measured range rules are needed.

Apply the highest difficulty called for by scope or complexity. A small, heavily enchanted lock can be harder than a large mundane one. You may narrow your intention before rolling to qualify for an easier exercise.

## Marking strength

A successful spell has the strength listed for its difficulty. Rolled face values determine matches; they are not added together for damage or healing.

If the **largest matching group exceeds the requirement**, add **one point of strength**, once per cast. A Routine spell with three or more alike therefore has strength 3. Separate pairs do not add strength. 

For utility spells without a useful point value, an extra-strong match makes the same effect especially clean or precise. It does not bypass an unrelated obstacle.

## Practical applications

### Attack

Deal the spell's strength in damage. For multiple targets, divide the total strength between them before drawing, assigning at least one point to each. A Demanding attack dealing 4 points might assign 2 to each of two targets, rather than dealing 4 to everyone.

If an oversized match grants the one-point bonus, assign it to one of those already declared targets after rolling. The same rule applies to divided shields and healing. Do not select a new target after seeing the result.

An unexpected mana expression can keep the same damage with a different description. Fire, solid words, icy pellets, or furious roots can all be attacks.

### Protection

Create a shield that absorbs damage up to its strength, then ends. It lasts until exhausted or the immediate scene ends. For several protected targets, divide the strength before drawing as with an attack.

Protection against a specific non-damaging hazard, such as smoke or a minor haunting, uses the same difficulty benchmarks instead of an absorption total. It protects against that declared hazard, not every possible consequence.

### Healing

Restore Composure equal to strength, up to the target's maximum. Divide healing between multiple targets before drawing. Green repairs living tissue; a wrong-color cast provides an unexpected benefit of comparable scale rather than automatically restoring Composure with every color.

For example, a White result might supply a protective air cushion around an injured student; Purple might hide them from a pursuing threat. Such substitutions must have a practical benefit, but they do not erase the injury. Restoring Composure to a Dazed student cancels infirmary collection as described in [[Turns Trouble and Recovery]]; concealing their distress alone does not.

### Movement and utility

Move, reveal, conceal, reshape, or otherwise resolve the declared obstacle at the agreed scope. Most utility effects do not need point totals.

- Routine: lift a key, illuminate a classroom, conceal one student, grow a foothold, or ask a cooperative ghost one question.
- Demanding: move three students across a gap, conceal a small group, reshape a heavy door, or uncover information through magical interference.
- Exceptional: move five students past a major barrier, reshape a large structure, or contain a major magical disturbance long enough to act.

Treat these as one immediate action or one scene of effect. Longer-lasting changes need an agreed limit before rolling. Recasting an unchanged divination question does not manufacture new information; the situation must provide something new to discover.

### Duration, restraint, and influence

Agree on duration before drawing. A scene means the current continuous situation with the same immediate stakes; moving into the next room does not automatically end pursuit, damage, or a troublesome enchantment. Ending and restarting a scene to refresh recovery or erase a consequence is not permitted.

- **Momentary distraction:** prevents one announced threat action when the description explains how. It ends after that threat phase. Repeating it requires another action and cast, and an intelligent threat can change tactics.
- **Ongoing restraint or concealment:** lasts through the scene, but its scope must fit the target. Restraining a nuisance can be Routine, a scene threat Demanding, and a major problem Exceptional. Threats can spend their announced action breaking an ordinary binding or searching; an effect strong enough to contain the whole threat follows its stated solution difficulty.
- **Influence:** wins one plausible concession at Routine scope. Compelling serious cooperation or changing a deeply held position is harder and must fit the scene; a Routine charm cannot resolve an entire quest by ordering everyone to obey.
- **Physical changes:** opened doors, moved objects, and repaired channels remain changed. Temporary enchantments expire at their agreed end. Ordinary environmental consequences require attention rather than vanishing solely because casting has stopped.

You may dismiss your own ongoing enchantment on your turn without another roll or meaningful action, provided it is still your controllable effect. Dismissal does not reverse damage, refund Composure, or undo a runaway Wild effect or backfire. Such trouble needs a suitable solution.

## When the ingredients disagree

Numerical success establishes how much magic takes hold. Mana determines its expression, using [[Spellcasting]]. A wrong-color result should retain the original purpose where possible; it may substitute a useful benefit when the new mana cannot reasonably accomplish that purpose.

A successful **Wild** result produces an unintended spell from the tied leading colors. Its scope may not exceed the agreed difficulty; its total points remain the recorded spell strength. The caster chooses the useful effect and complication under [[Spellcasting#Explaining an unintended result|Explaining an unintended result]]. A successful Wild result is not a failed examination.

Example: a Routine Red/Green tie on Fire Sword creates a flaming rosebush. It can threaten the target with 2 damage, but then obstructs the caster's route. It does not deal full damage every turn indefinitely.

## Incident grading

If the largest matching group falls short of difficulty, the intended spell fails and a backfire occurs. Use the leading mana color to theme it; a leading-color tie produces a Wild backfire combining those colors.

Compare the required group with the largest rolled group. If no faces match, that largest group is one.

| Shortfall | Severity | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 die short | Minor | One brief complication or 1 damage to the caster |
| 2 dice short | Serious | One scene-lasting complication or 2 damage to the caster |
| 3 dice short | Spectacular | A major scene problem or 3 damage to the caster |

Record a complication **or** damage as the principal consequence, not both automatically. Soot and missing eyebrows may accompany either without extra penalties. Serious trouble must leave classmates something useful to do; it is not grounds for excluding a student from the entire practical.

A backfire does not also deal successful attack damage to the intended target. Ingredients still return to the pouch. Matching faces below difficulty do not turn a failed spell into a successful one.

| Blue — Water / Binding | The caster's shoelaces knot together until untied. | The caster is tethered to a nearby object until the binding is broken. | A local flood carries loose objects and blocks the planned route. |

Staff attention can follow a visible or noisy mishap when it makes sense in the scene. Use the shared Faculty Suspicion track in [[Threats and Faculty]]; do not add automatic Suspicion for every backfire.

## Repeating an exercise

Returning ingredients permits repeated spells. During a pressured scene, each attempt should consume an action or meaningful opportunity; a failed attempt changes the situation through its backfire. A later attempt is a new cast, not a free retry.

Outside pressured scenes, Routine spells with no meaningful stakes can simply work as intended with the agreement of your Professor. Roll when uncertainty, an unexpected expression, or a consequence matters. During safe downtime, healing can restore Composure up to its maximum. See [[Turns Trouble and Recovery]] for rest and recovery.

A consequence-free automatic action does not grant the oversized-match bonus. For magical protection or other preparations before danger, establish the situation first: if the mana result could matter, make the cast. Time spent repeating attempts gives an active threat or approaching deadline an opportunity to advance. Do not freely stockpile shields; the non-stacking rule applies throughout the continuous scene.
