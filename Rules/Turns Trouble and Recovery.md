# Conduct During an Incident

Consult: [[Spellcasting]] | [[Spell Effects and Backfires]] | [[Students and Advancement]] | [[PRISMS]]

> [!quote] Student incident report
> Nobody was seriously hurt. Several people are now considerably easier to identify.

## A note from Nurse Bell

You are sturdier than you think. Ordinary practical accidents leave soot, missing eyebrows, ringing ears, and offended clothing. They may also leave stories your classmates remember for years. Our welfare wards handle exhaustion and magical battering; Nurse Bell prefers an embarrassed student to an injured one.

Every student has **10 Composure**, representing their ability to withstand magical battering and exhaustion. It does not increase with level; advancement improves casting and ingredient control. Damage, healing, and shields use the strengths in [[Spell Effects and Backfires]].

Damage reduces Composure. Record an appropriate visible symptom: a charred silhouette, a wet hat, a voice an octave too high. These signs add no penalty by themselves. If something is actually in your way, describe what it stops you doing.

## Taking your turn in a practical

State where you are in relation to what matters: beside the cauldron, across the greenhouse, behind the hedge, or beyond the locked door. Describe whether you can reach the runic circle or what stands in your way. You do not need to measure the distance.

During ordinary instruction, speak and act naturally, allowing classmates to contribute. When danger, pursuit, or a deadline requires orderly action, the Professor calls for rounds:

1. Students act in an order they agree on; everyone gets one turn.
2. Each turn allows **one meaningful action** plus ordinary movement and brief speech.
3. After the students act, resolve the announced threat action for the scene, or its two announced actions in a finale. Use the shared action budget in [[Threats and Faculty]], rather than granting each creature a separate turn.

Agree on student order at the start of each round. A student may delay until later in that student phase, but never gains a second action or carries an unused action into the next round. A student restored from Dazed may act only if their turn has not yet occurred; recovery does not grant an extra turn. Students do not gain interrupting reactions or out-of-turn defense rolls in practical exercises.

The Professor tells you what danger you can see coming before you take your turns. If an incident has already caught you by surprise, resolve that first event, then begin rounds.

A meaningful action can be casting, tackling a risky task, helping, freeing someone, or dealing with a complication. Ordinary movement is free when you can safely make it as part of the action. Crossing a dangerous gap, forcing a blocked route, or escaping a pursuer is itself a meaningful action and may need a draw.

Before drawing, agree whether the intended target is reachable from the described position. A spell that crosses a major obstacle or affects an entire area may need higher difficulty. 

## Applying your studies to everyday difficulties

You have spent a term learning spells. Naturally, every stuck door looks like an opportunity to prove it. Practical credit also requires evidence of magical application.

For **any risky task, make a blind draw from your pouch**. That includes sneaking, climbing, persuasion, and investigation. Explain the small magical technique accompanying your effort. Draw five ordinarily, or your specialty allowance when the technique fits your school of choice. Follow [[Spellcasting]] for matches and every drawn color.

Examples:

| Task | Student's magical approach | Possible school |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sneak past a teacher | Conceal their silhouette or soften their footsteps | Illusion or Abjuration |
| Climb to a window | Reshape handholds or summon a helping vine | Transmutation or Conjuration |
| Persuade a classmate | Supply an impressive magical demonstration or lend their voice extra warmth | Evocation or Enchantment |
| Search a classroom | Ask a locating charm to point out disturbed objects | Divination |
| Lift a stuck hatch | Reinforce their arms or conjure a pulling connection | Transmutation or Conjuration |
| Ask about a suspicious noise | Consult the corridor's resident ghost | Necromancy |

Declare the intended color as well as the technique. A wrong-color success can still accomplish the task in an unexpected way. A successful Blue sneaking effect might quietly slide the student behind a curtain; a successful Brown result might hide them behind a suddenly erected partition. A Wild result provides an unintended opportunity and complication, rather than automatic stealth.

If you're trying to manage without casting, explain the reflex charm, stray spark, or old enchantment that gets involved anyway. Use the same draw. Your magic doesn't always wait to be asked.

Simple actions without meaningful uncertainty need no draw: walking through an open door, carrying a normal book, or talking to a willing friend. Students can use their hands. The bag comes out when the outcome matters.

## Why magical application is required

There is usually a reason to reach for your pouch, even when a broom would do:

- The marking rubric requires a demonstration of the student's specialty.
- The mundane equipment is locked away after last term's incident.
- The object is enchanted and ignores ordinary handling.
- Time is short, and a spell offers an immediate solution.
- A student wants to prove that they have finally mastered the technique.

Save draws for uncertain tasks that matter. Sweeping an ordinary floor still needs only a broom.

## Assistance between students

Spend your action describing a useful contribution and name the classmate and task it supports. Their next relevant draw gains **one extra blind-drawn die from their own pouch**, before any dice are rolled. The bonus expires at the end of the next student phase, is consumed by that draw, and cannot stack with another helper bonus. Helping yourself does not qualify. The helper does not make a second roll. In an unpressured scene, the bonus applies to the next immediate attempt at the named task.

Helping can also solve a concrete problem directly: brush flowers from someone's eyes, untie shoelaces, or pull a classmate from a hedge. If there is no remaining uncertainty, that action succeeds without a roll.

## Visible symptoms and actual impediments

Some mishaps only change your appearance. Others need dealing with:

- **Cosmetic:** missing eyebrows, green hair, a smoking hat, or a squeaky voice. These attract comment and recognition by staff; they carry no automatic dice penalty.
- **Brief trouble:** tangled laces, flowers over the eyes, or an inconveniently hot tool. Spend a suitable action to clear it, or let a classmate help.
- **Scene trouble:** rooted clothes, an animated desk, or an exit hidden by illusion. A fitting magical action or clever physical solution can remove it. If left alone, it ends with the scene unless its description clearly establishes an ongoing problem.

Write down what's actually in your way and say how you'll deal with it. You don't need a list of numerical penalties. Rooted boots prevent walking away; they do not prevent speaking, casting, or examining what remains within reach.

## Protective arrangements

A student uses only the **strongest remaining magical shield** on them. New shields replace it only if stronger; they do not stack. Subtract incoming damage from the shield first, applying any excess to Composure. Shields expire when exhausted or the scene ends.

A spell that sticks around doesn't keep dealing damage for free. A flaming rosebush can remain an obstacle, but dealing spell damage again requires another action and cast. Environmental hazards use the scene's normal threat action.

## Dazed students and collection wards

At **zero Composure**, a student is **Dazed**, rather than seriously injured. Composure cannot fall below zero. They are conscious enough to talk and describe their predicament, but cannot take normal meaningful actions until helped.

The school's ward announces its intention to collect them: a glowing collection number, a hovering pillow, or a cheerful voice saying, "Please remain where you have collapsed."

Collection happens at the **end of the next student phase after the phase in which the student became Dazed**. If they fall during a student phase, classmates may help immediately and throughout the following student phase. If they fall during a threat phase, the following student phase is the response window. That gives the whole group a chance to help. An unrecovered student is then safely teleported to the infirmary, before the next threat phase.

- **Quick assistance:** an adjacent classmate spends an action offering water, encouragement, or a steadying hand. No roll is needed when they can safely reach the student. Restore **1 Composure** and cancel the collection. If reaching them is itself dangerous, resolve that obstacle first.
- **Magical healing:** a successful healing effect restores its normal amount and cancels collection if it raises Composure above zero. Wrong-color protection alone does not restore their Composure.
- **Infirmary collection:** the student recovers to full Composure and returns at the next sensible scene transition. Staff now know something happened and may require an explanation, impose supervision, or assign corrective homework.

Damage while Dazed does not drive Composure below zero or restart the collection timer. Prefer another active target or a scene complication rather than repeatedly battering a student who cannot act. Quick assistance only restores a Dazed student; it is not unlimited one-point healing for students already above zero. If a revived student becomes Dazed again, begin a new collection window.

Keep collected students in the story. Bring them back promptly, play out their conversation with Nurse Bell, or move between the infirmary and the adventure. The wards keep students safe; the awkward questions are what make collection inconvenient.

## Rest and the return of composure

After a pressured scene, students who can take a few quiet minutes recover **2 Composure**, up to 10. A proper safe rest, meal, or infirmary visit restores all Composure. Healing can also restore Composure during safe downtime, up to 10.

The two-point breather applies once after that pressured scene, not once per pause or room entered. A safe full rest requires the immediate threat or deadline to be resolved or genuinely escaped. If the entire cohort is Dazed, incapacitated, or collected, proceed directly to ward rescue and a supervised continuation. Resume once the students can take part again.

Recovery clears exhaustion, not every visible sign of a mishap. Missing eyebrows and ink-stained uniforms can survive long enough to complicate the debrief. You and your Professor may agree when a cosmetic symptom has served its purpose. Eyebrows do grow back.
