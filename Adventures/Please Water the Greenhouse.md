
# Assignment One: Please Water the Greenhouse

An unauthorized expedition for incoming Level 1 students. Its resolution counts as your first major practical at debrief, whether or not anyone approved your departure.

Consult: [[Threats and Faculty]] | [[Spellcasting]] | [[Students and Advancement]] | [[Turns Trouble and Recovery]]

> [!quote] Note passed beneath a worksheet
> Moss is away and something in the greenhouse is crying for water. I've borrowed the key. Quill's asleep. Please hurry before it gets any louder.

## Instructions to students

Begin in afternoon study with your twelve-die permanent allowance. Quill has issued the **six-die Mixed Practical Supplement** for today's exercises; mix it into your one pouch, giving eighteen dice. The packet smells of wet pennies. Quill is now resting his eyes over a worksheet entitled Responsible Magical Application.

Someone passes you the note and a borrowed key. Slip out to find out what is happening and help the thirsty specimens. Your ordinary draw is five; your Level 1 specialty draw is seven. The watering cans remain locked away following an earlier incident. The irrigation apparatus accepts several forms of magic, so apply your department's instruction rather than waiting for a particular color.

Try to return to class before Quill notices the empty chairs, and return the key before supper. Groundskeeper Moss is elsewhere on campus. Nurse Bell's collection wards remain active even when you are somewhere you should not be. Any shrub claiming legal representation should be referred to the Office of Institute Affairs.

At debrief, your cohort reaches Level 2. Return the packet and retain two prepared supplies in chosen colors, for fourteen permanent dice, at most seven of one color.

> [!warning] Sealed supervisor's appendix
> The remaining material contains specimen records and concealed causes. Reserved for the Professor. Students are instructed to stop reading here. The Office acknowledges that instruction may be insufficient.


> [!Warning]- Supervisor's preparation
>
> - Confirm admission records and twelve-die starting allowances.
> - Read each student's background knack and outstanding business in [[Students and Advancement#Previous experience and outstanding business|Previous experience and outstanding business]]. Weave a connection into the assignment using the arrangements below.
> - Give each student six random unwanted quest dice following [[Students and Advancement]]. Mix them into the existing pouch: one working pool of 18 dice per student.
> - Begin everyone at 10 Composure and shared Suspicion 0.
> - Confirm that students know their five-die ordinary and seven-die specialty allowances.
> - Use fixed effect strengths of 2, 4, and 6 for spell effects.
> - Threat totals below assume three students. Apply the cohort-size adjustment in [[Threats and Faculty]] at each encounter's start: for four students, topiary Stability is 8 and root Stability is 13.
>
> The quest dice come from the **Mixed Practical Supplement** compulsory for the assignment. Professor Quill insists that learning to use unfamiliar ingredients is the point. Nobody has successfully explained why the packet smells of wet pennies.
> 
> ## Assignment background
>
> Professor Quill has assigned quiet classroom exercises and fallen asleep. A fellow student has heard distress from the greenhouse and borrowed its key. Tie that student or the thirsty specimens to one of the cohort's background connections where suitable; otherwise use a worried classmate. The students choose how to leave and investigate.
>
> The ordinary watering cans are locked away after an earlier incident. An enchanted irrigation system accepts magical input, allowing every school to contribute: reveal a dry bed, protect seedlings, reshape a channel, coax a valve, or animate a watering vessel.
>
> Groundskeeper Moss is elsewhere on campus. Staff expect the students to be in class, not handling this problem themselves.
>
> Unknown to staff, the irrigation system is connected to a buried root drinking the greenhouse wards. Activating the system wakes the root regardless of the first numerical result. Record a successful cast honestly; the underlying fault is present before the students arrive.
>
> ## Arrangements for students with previous experience
>
> Adapt one or two personal connections to this practical and keep the others available for later assignments. Invite their students to help establish the details. These are opportunities alongside the shared task, not compulsory additional obstacles.
>
> - A former greenhouse assistant recognizes Moss's irrigation labels without a roll; their troublesome seedling has been placed in the Faculty Favorite bed and wants rescuing.
> - A kitchen regular finds the cook's missing recipe tucked into the complaining rosebush's paperwork. The shrub requests a correction to the phrase "edible garnish."
> - A chronic sneak recognizes a confiscation label on a keepsake beneath the potting shed. Retrieving it can accompany making the conduit safe.
> - A former athlete finds their teammate's wandering boots caught in the topiary. The boots demand an explanation of the offside rule before agreeing to leave.
>
> Use the student's actual outstanding business rather than assigning these examples automatically. Familiarity can provide ordinary information without a roll; it does not reveal the buried root's magical fault by itself. Resolving a connection offers a changed relationship or a future lead, not extra reward dice. Leave the common clues and solutions available to everyone.
>
> ## Departure: Quietly, if possible
>
> Describe Quill's gentle snoring, spectacles facing the desks, and a corridor beyond the classroom door. Let the students plan their departure. A clear, safe opening needs no roll; if a prefect or watchful enchantment makes escape uncertain, use a normal task cast with the difficulty agreed beforehand. No separate stealth system or sleeping-professor roll is needed.
>
> An unexpected success still gets the students a useful way out; Wild magic also brings a complication. A backfire changes the situation rather than closing the adventure. Raise Suspicion only if the incident leaves observable evidence, and let the cohort continue with a less convincing cover story or staff beginning to look for them.
>
> ## Inspection stage: A perfectly ordinary greenhouse
>
> Describe sweating windows, labeled beds, an argumentative watering vessel, and a brass wheel beside a gauge reading **Ward Pressure: Adequate**.
>
> Invite each student to explain their approach. Safe observation needs no roll. Require a draw for consequential magical work or uncertain handling.
>
> Possible discoveries:
>
> - Looking carefully reveals three beds marked **Dry**, **Drier**, and **Faculty Favorite**.
> - A Routine revealing or investigative action discovers a pipe leading underneath the potting shed.
> - A Routine task cast can water or otherwise support one bed using suitable magic.
> - A Demanding cast can address all three beds at once.
>
> Unexpected colors should offer usable alternatives: Green roots carry moisture, Brown reshapes channels, White gathers mist, Purple convinces the enchanted vessel to follow instructions. Do not require Blue dominance to finish the assignment.
>
> After the first consequential interaction with irrigation, the pressure gauge drops, the floor trembles, and a polite shrub announces that it has retained counsel. Move into pressured rounds.
>
> ## Incident stage: The specimens have concerns
>
> The awakened root disrupts irrigation. A thorny topiary guards the brass wheel while a rosebush tries to read a formal complaint aloud.
>
> ### Territorial topiary — Scene trouble
>
> - **Stability:** 6.
> - **Want:** keep students away from the wheel.
> - **Warning:** its branches curl around a watering pot and aim it at a student.
> - **Action:** fling the pot for 2 damage, or block the route with a thorny branch.
> - **Way out:** distract it with a better gardening task, magically restrain it, or satisfy its demand for an explanation.
>
> Use one threat action per round. The complaining rosebush supplies dialogue rather than a second attack.
>
> Clues are available without a specific school: the gauge visibly drains when the root pulses; a pipe shakes toward the shed; the topiary complains that "the thing downstairs is taking everyone's dinner." A successful Routine investigative action identifies the connection immediately. If investigation backfires, still leave the visible clues available.
>
> Students can bypass or calm the topiary with a suitable Demanding effect, exhaust it through attacks, or create an opportunity with a Routine distraction and follow through. A successful Wild effect changes the scene: a singing hedge distracts the topiary but broadcasts the cohort location, for example.
>
> ## Investigation stage: Beneath the potting shed
>
> The cohort discovers a root wrapped around a ward conduit. It thinks the school's protective magic is fertilizer.
>
> ### Ward-drinking root — Major problem
>
> - **Stability:** 10.
> - **Want:** consume the conduit's energy and grow into the greenhouse above.
> - **Warning:** a root coils around a student while another tendril tightens over the conduit.
> - **Action:** squeeze one student for 3 damage, or advance a visible growth problem by blocking a route.
> - **Way out:** disconnect it, redirect its food source, persuade its awakened presence to cooperate, or contain it while repairing the conduit.
>
> Begin with one action per round. If the incident requires additional urgency, announce two distinct warnings before the next student phase; keep the second action a complication rather than more damage for this introductory finale.
>
> Offer several solutions:
>
> - **Two Demanding steps:** isolate the conduit, then move or redirect the root. Successful steps remain accomplished; do not reset progress after a later backfire.
> - **One Exceptional effect:** contain the whole disturbance for the scene, allowing ordinary safe disconnection afterward.
> - **Direct effort:** reduce Stability to zero with spells, describing the root as exhausted or disentangled rather than requiring it to be killed.
> - **An inventive plan:** set its difficulty from the effect benchmarks. Useful preparation can make one part Routine, or grant the normal one-helper die on a consequential cast.
>
> Do not require exact colors for these solutions. The technique and result must make sense, but different mana expressions can produce different ways forward.
>
> ## Intervention arrangements
>
> Use [[Threats and Faculty]] when an incident creates observable evidence. A noisy Wild result may raise Suspicion; a quiet one need not. Infirmary collection adds one for that incident and ensures staff know someone needs checking on.
>
> If Suspicion reaches 6, Groundskeeper Moss arrives with a recently awakened Professor Quill. They stabilize any immediate danger and ask why watering the greenhouse required leaving class without permission. The students finish isolating the root under supervision. Faculty intervention changes the circumstances and creates a consequence, rather than solving every remaining task.
>
> If the entire cohort is collected, staff secure the greenhouse while students recover. Arrange their prompt supervised return to diagnose and repair the connection. The quest can still conclude, and students still earn advancement.
>
> ## Marking and debrief
>
> The expedition concludes when the conduit is safe, the beds are attended to, and the students return to class or account for their absence. The key must be returned or its loss explained. Repair duty can be folded into this final scene. Quill recognizes the work as a major practical at debrief; practical credit does not erase consequences for sneaking out.
>
> Ask each student:
>
> - What did you intend to do?
> - What did your ingredients actually do?
> - What would you change about your pouch?
>
> Each student advances to **Level 2**, returns the original six-die packet, and receives two permanent dice in chosen colors. Their permanent pouches now contain **14 dice**, with no color exceeding seven. This represents keeping two prepared portions and returning four surplus supplies. Reset Suspicion after the debrief and carry forward any agreed faculty consequence.
>
> File the unresolved shrub complaint for possible follow-up instruction. Do not promise it a hearing before supper.
