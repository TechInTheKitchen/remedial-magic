# 03 - Please Return the Library Books

[[Remedial Magic|Contents]] | [[Professor (DM)/02 - Please Water the Greenhouse|Previous]]

[[Players/06 - Please Return the Library Books|Instructions to students]]

> [!warning] Sealed supervisor's appendix
> The rest of this assignment is for the Professor. Students: stop here to keep the library's problems a surprise.

## Preparing the adventure

This follows the greenhouse assignment and assumes **three Level 2 students**. It is slightly harder: the first obstacle calls for a Demanding solution, and the finale begins with two announced threat actions. Investigation and preparation can reduce that pressure. There are no new dice rules or hidden countdowns.

- Start each student at **10 Composure**, with **14 permanent dice** and an **eight-die practical packet** mixed into the same pouch: **22 dice** before other additions. Draw the packet using the usual preferred-color exclusions and half-pouch limit.
- Ordinary draws are **five dice**; specialty draws are **eight**. Spell strengths remain **2 / 4 / 6**, with the usual one-point bonus for exceeding the required match.
- Start shared Faculty Suspicion at **0**. Carry forward any actual supervision or repair duty from the previous adventure; do not quietly cancel it to make sneaking out easier.
- Scale threat totals using [[Professor (DM)/01 - Running the Game#Troublesome Specimens and Faculty Attention|Running the Game]]. The trolley has **6 Stability**, adjusted by 2 per student above or below three, minimum 2. The recall enchantment has **10 Stability**, adjusted by 3 per student, minimum 4. Four students face totals of 8 and 13; two face 4 and 7.
- Read the students' unfinished business. Choose a connection to Nell and one object the library might mistakenly recall. Ask the relevant player for the details.

### What has gone wrong

The library's brass recall bell brings overdue books back to the returns desk. Its instructions come from an enchanted ledger in the gallery above the reading room.

A leaking roof has soaked the ledger. Ink has spread from **Books on loan** into **Borrowers** and **Library property**. The bell is now calling back readers, chairs, and anything bearing a library stamp. Nell's receipt lists her as an unreturned item. This is a damaged enchantment, not Nell's fault.

The ledger has wrapped itself in loose pages and book straps. It is trying to complete every return at once. The repair needs three things: stop the bell transmitting, correct the entries, and release the existing recall bindings. Earlier discoveries can make that work easier.

### People and personal connections

**Nell** wants to get back to class without being shelved. She is frightened but observant. She can describe the desk printing her name, the damp smell upstairs, and the bell pulling harder with each test ring. She never withholds an essential clue while waiting for a successful persuasion roll.

Use one or two of these connections, adapted to the actual students:

- A kitchen regular recognizes the cook's recipe book on the trolley. Its library stamp is old; the cook believed it had been given to her.
- A chronic sneak sees a friend's confiscated keepsake being dragged along by a library receipt stuck to its wrapping. Removing it safely resolves that small problem without a draw.
- A former athlete finds a teammate holding a door open against a bench being recalled from the changing rooms. They need help, not a lecture about borrowing furniture.
- A greenhouse assistant recognizes the leaf used to blot the wet ledger. Moss donated the plant, but nobody asked whether its sap would dissolve ink.

Familiar experience supplies ordinary information without rolling. Leave the main clues and repair routes available to everyone. Personal connections can change through play; they do not grant extra permanent dice.

## Scene One The library entrance

Read or paraphrase:

> The library doors stand open. Inside, a long runner rug has bunched against the returns desk. A chair bumps down the corridor on its back legs, stops at the threshold, and waits behind three floating books. Somewhere above you, a bell gives one low note. Every book in the queue turns its spine toward the sound.

The air smells of furniture polish and wet paper. A sign over the desk says **Please return only what you borrowed**. Someone has written **including students?** underneath it.

Students can leave Quill's room safely when there is a clear opening. Use a normal task cast only if a prefect, supervision, or another established obstacle makes departure risky. No separate sleep check is needed. If staff accompany them, run the same problem as supervised work.

At the entrance, give these details through ordinary observation:

- Each recalled object has a glowing receipt attached. The light pulses toward the gallery when the bell sounds.
- The returns slot has printed Nell's name under **Item description**.
- An open maintenance drawer contains a diagram labeled **Bell -> Ledger -> Return bindings**. Three arrows connect them in that order.
- The doorway remains a safe retreat. The recall pulls stamped objects toward the desk; it is not a general force that drags everyone into the building.

A Routine investigative cast can trace Nell's receipt to the reading room or identify the bell's transmission cable. A failed investigation adds its normal backfire, but the printed receipt and maintenance diagram remain available. Let safe inspection reveal them without a roll.

## Scene Two The returns trolley

Read or paraphrase:

> Nell is standing on a reading table with a stack of dictionaries in her arms. A receipt stretches from her sleeve to a brass trolley below. Its wheels keep turning against the table leg. Around her, the reading chairs have arranged themselves in a neat queue. One chair still has someone's schoolbag on it.

The reading room has high windows, green-shaded lamps, and a narrow stair leading to the gallery. Bookcases give solid cover. The trolley can pursue along the open aisles but cannot pass through a bookcase or climb the stair. A student who reaches the far side of a solid bookcase can avoid a charge announced down the original aisle.

Nell's dictionaries are weighing down the receipt. She can hold on while the students assess the room; do not start with an unavoidable attack.

### Overloaded returns trolley — Scene trouble

- **Stability:** 6 before cohort adjustment.
- **Want:** take Nell and every stamped item to the returns slot.
- **Warning:** its front wheels lift and its handle points down a named aisle toward one student.
- **Action:** charge that student for **2 damage**, or wind a receipt around a student's ankle, tethering them to the trolley until freed.
- **Way out:** suspend its collection instructions, detach Nell's recall binding, divert it into the empty returns bay, or exhaust its enchantment.

Use **one threat action for the whole scene**, including any moving chairs. A charge deals damage or creates a complication, never both. The trolley does not get a replacement attack if students prevent its announced action.

Ways through include:

- **Demanding:** free Nell from the active recall binding, disable the trolley for the scene, or redirect the whole queue into the empty returns bay. Different schools and colors can accomplish these in different ways.
- **Routine preparation:** distract the trolley for one threat phase or jam one aisle while a classmate gets into position. This creates an opening; it does not permanently end the recall.
- **Physical plan:** wedge the trolley against a sturdy bookcase, then work on the receipt. Safe placement needs no roll; handling it while it charges is a risky magical task. Agree on the difficulty for the actual plan.
- **Attacks:** reduce Stability to zero. The trolley stops; Nell's receipt loosens enough to remove safely. The school-wide recall still needs repairing upstairs.

A wrong-color success must still help. A Wild success may free Nell while sticking the receipt to the student's hat, for example; it should not immediately undo the rescue.

When the trolley is contained, Nell and the students can take a **safe breather for 2 Composure**, once for this pressured scene. The gallery door and a heavy bookcase can keep the room secure while they rest. A full rest needs a real escape from the immediate problem; offer fetching staff as an option.

## Scene Three The quiet gallery

This scene comes **before the final encounter**. Give students time to look, talk, and prepare. Do not jump straight from the trolley to another attack.

Read or paraphrase:

> Upstairs, the carpet muffles your footsteps. Rain taps against a cracked skylight, and a bucket underneath it has overflowed. Water runs along a shelf into an open ledger. At the far end of the gallery, beyond a glass door, loose pages rise and settle around a brass bell. Each time its clapper twitches, the wet ink in the ledger moves too.

The gallery overlooks the reading room. Below, Nell is clearing a place for anyone who needs to retreat. Nearby are a maintenance bench, a dry reading alcove, and a display of old library notices. The glass door has a clear view into the bell chamber and a handle on this side. It is not locked.

Give these clues when students inspect the relevant objects. No single roll is required to understand the repair:

| Place | What students find | What it tells them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maintenance bench | A diagram with a felt cover drawn around the bell's clapper | Stopping the bell stops new recall orders, but existing bindings remain. |
| Old library notices | A sample return entry: book title, borrower, then **Received** | The ledger needs to distinguish books from people and mark the correct returns. |
| Glass door | Receipt threads pass through a brass release ring beneath the lectern | The existing bindings can be released after the ledger is corrected. |
| Wet shelf | The original headings remain legible under the smeared ink | The error can be repaired; the students do not need to invent a new system. |

### Useful preparations

Tell the students what each preparation will accomplish before they attempt it. They may do any or all of these; none requires a particular mana color.

- **Make a bell cover.** A folded felt book wrap and its fitting instructions are on the bench. Assembling them safely needs no roll. In the finale, fitting the prepared cover becomes a **Routine** task instead of a Demanding one. Getting close to the bell still takes an action and may expose the student to its announced threat.
- **Prepare the corrected entries.** Comparing the sample with Nell's receipt reveals the error without a roll. A **Routine** magical task makes a dry, legible replacement page or separates the original ink. With that page ready, correcting the ledger in the finale becomes **Routine** instead of Demanding. A backfire leaves the sample readable and the Demanding repair available.
- **Find a sheltered approach.** Safe observation reveals a stone pillar beside the lectern. It shields one student from a paper volley coming from the bell end of the room. It does not block the receipt threads. Explain this position before anyone enters.

Let inventive preparation have a similarly concrete benefit. It can make one repair step easier or justify the usual helper die; do not stack extra dice from several preparations. Preparing a spell shield uses the normal casting rules and non-stacking limit.

The bell's uneven test notes provide atmosphere, not a secret timer. If students spend a long time repeating spells, describe a new receipt creeping out under the glass door and announce the approaching trouble before it acts. Do not deal off-screen damage or raise Suspicion just because they investigate.

## Final encounter The recall bell

Before the students enter, describe the entire chamber:

> The bell hangs over a waist-high lectern. Wet pages circle it like a flock of birds. To the left, a stone pillar divides the open floor from a narrow maintenance walkway. Under the lectern, dozens of receipt threads pass through a brass ring. The stair door stays behind you. A fresh line writes itself across the ledger: **Whole class — not yet returned**.

The bell, ledger, and receipt threads are **one connected threat**, not three creatures. The ledger is open on the lectern, below the bell and within reach of someone standing beside it. The release ring is beneath it. Students can get there from the doorway with ordinary movement while the route is clear. A blocked route must be dealt with first. There is no unavoidable opening hit.

### Runaway recall enchantment — Major problem

- **Stability:** 10 before cohort adjustment, shared by the whole enchantment.
- **Want:** complete every return listed in the damaged ledger.
- **Warning one:** loose pages fold into stiff wedges and point at a named exposed student.
- **Action one:** a paper volley deals **3 damage to one target**. The stone pillar blocks a volley from the bell end of the room. The pages cannot turn that announced volley around the pillar halfway through the phase.
- **Warning two:** a receipt thread tightens across a named route or winds toward a student's sleeve.
- **Action two:** block that route with bound books, or tether that student to the lectern until freed. This action deals **no damage**.
- **Way out:** stop new orders, correct the ledger, then release the existing bindings; contain the whole disturbance; or exhaust the enchantment through attacks.

Announce **both actions before student turns**, including their targets and affected route. Use no additional damage from the bell, moving furniture, wet floor, or other scenery. Stopping one action leaves only the other; never replace a prevented action with an unannounced one.

### Repairing the system

Each step takes one meaningful action and a suitable cast unless a plan has made the handling safe and ordinary. Students can work together, but complete the steps in order:

1. **Stop the bell transmitting — Demanding, or Routine with the prepared cover.** Muffle the clapper, isolate its transmission, or otherwise stop new recall orders. This removes the receipt-thread action for the rest of the encounter. Existing bindings and the circling pages remain.
2. **Correct the ledger — Demanding, or Routine with a prepared replacement page.** Restore the distinction between books and borrowers. The pages stop attacking once this succeeds. A consequence already in place, such as a blocked route or tether, still needs clearing.
3. **Release the bindings — Routine.** With no active threat, opening the clearly labeled ring by hand is safe and needs no roll if the students can reach it. If a remaining complication makes access risky, resolve that obstacle or use a suitable Routine cast. Nell and the recalled objects are released.

Keep successful repairs completed. A later backfire adds its own consequence but does not erase earlier work. A wrong-color result can count as a repair if its useful effect actually achieves the step. If it supplies a different benefit instead, state that benefit and leave the step available; do not silently mark it finished or treat the successful cast as a backfire.

### Other solutions

- **Exceptional containment:** contain the whole enchantment for the scene. Both threat actions stop while the containment lasts. Students can then carry out the known repairs by safe ordinary handling, provided access is clear. This is an alternative to several pressured casts.
- **Direct attacks:** reducing shared Stability to zero exhausts the recall enchantment. Both actions stop and the receipts release their targets. The students can dry the ledger and correct its entries safely afterward; do not demand another battle to finish.
- **A different plan:** use the normal scope benchmarks. A Routine distraction prevents one explained threat action for one phase; it does not turn off the whole major threat. An appropriate Exceptional solution can settle the whole incident.

If the group is struggling, make the remaining opportunities clear: the pillar, prepared cover, visible release mechanism, and retreat to Nell's secured room. Staff help is always possible. Keep the challenge in their choices and the announced danger, not in guessing the right sentence.

## Specimens and staff attention

Offer usable specimens after meaningful progress, within the usual **one die per student per significant scene, three per assignment** limit:

- The contained trolley releases a spare **Blue binding thread**.
- The gallery's dry alcove holds discarded **Purple dream ink** suitable for use with the librarian's permission; Nell knows it is in the free-supplies tray.
- The repaired bell sheds a little **White warding chalk** from its mounting.

Announce the color before acceptance, check the half-pouch limit against the enlarged pouch, record the color, and mix it into the student's own pouch. These are optional temporary supplies. Do not require harvesting or offer repeated rewards for the same source.

Use the normal [[Players/04 - Trouble and Recovery#Faculty attention|Faculty Suspicion]] track. A crash heard in the corridor, recalled furniture crossing a staffed area, or an infirmary collection can leave clear evidence. One incident adds one Suspicion even if several things break. The ordinary warning bell is part of the existing fault; it does not add Suspicion every round.

At Suspicion 2–3, a prefect follows the scattered receipts. At 4–5, the librarian arrives at the entrance and calls up for an explanation. At 6, staff intervene, impose one fitting consequence, and reset Suspicion to 3. Let students complete a remaining repair under supervision. Staff can hold a volley or secure the approach without resolving every task for them.

If corrective supplies are chosen as that consequence, issue two random unwanted dice per implicated student between casts, using the normal exclusions and half-pouch limit. The cap remains four corrective dice per student for the assignment. Do not add these automatically to a different consequence.

If the entire group is Dazed or collected, staff contain the bell while they recover. Bring them back promptly to diagnose the wet ledger and complete safe repairs. This still counts as the major practical.

## Return and debrief

Conclude when the recall is stopped, Nell is free, and the students return or account for their absence. Ask where the furniture belongs, who owns the borrowed books, and who will repair the skylight. Restoring an object to the right person can settle or complicate a student's unfinished business.

Each student advances to **Level 3**. Return the **eight-die practical packet**, all collected specimens, and all corrective supplies by their recorded color counts. Then add **two permanent dice in chosen colors**. The permanent allowance becomes **16 dice**, with a maximum of eight of any color. Specialty draws become **nine**; ordinary draws remain five. Reset Suspicion to 0 and carry forward any agreed consequence.

Nell offers to return the cohort's next set of library books. She would prefer someone to come with her.
