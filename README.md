# Remedial Magic

*A rules-light tabletop RPG of summer school, questionable ingredients, and surprisingly durable students.*

Welcome to **Pengorgon's Remedial Institute for Summer Magical Studies**, or **PRISMS**. Your last magical mishap earned you a summer of supervised instruction. Fortunately, Professor Quill is supervising through his eyelids. Slip out with your classmates, follow whispered rumors, help friends, and investigate things the school insists are perfectly ordinary. Get back before he wakes. Preferably with the same number of eyebrows.

![A small broken gold wand over a plum-colored pattern of magical books, herbs, and symbols](assets/images/broken-wand-dark.webp)

## Magic from a mixed pouch

Each student casts from **one pouch of colored six-sided dice**. Matching faces determine whether the spell succeeds; every drawn color contributes to what kind of magic appears. You wanted a Fire Sword. Your ingredients supplied **Fires Word**. The target is now being attacked by solid language.

- **Build your pouch:** choose a magical specialty, earn ingredients, and refine your mix across five levels. Compulsory unfamiliar supplies keep accomplished students guessing.
- **Invent the surprises:** casters choose unexpected spells and backfires. Successful Wild magic brings a useful effect and a complication; classmates offer ideas when needed, and the Professor chooses from those suggestions.
- **Bring unfinished business:** one background knack ties your student to people, promises, and problems that can become adventures.
- **Keep things moving:** describe the scene rather than measuring ranges. Students are hearty, recovery is forgiving, and missing eyebrows are an educational outcome.

One player serves as the **Professor**, who runs the game; everyone else plays a student. Bring colored D6s, one opaque pouch per student, and a willingness to explain yourself. Professor Quill is the sleepy teacher in the story, not a requirement that the person running the game take a nap.

> “We were helping” is a good beginning. It is rarely a complete explanation.
