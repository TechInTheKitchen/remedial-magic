## Your First Afternoon at Prisms

*An orientation for incoming students. Margin notes have been retained where removal would damage the paper.*

Welcome to **Pengorgon's Remedial Institute for Summer Magical Studies**. You may call us **Prisms**. You're here because of an incident, an unfortunate exam result, or a building nobody can live in anymore. This summer, we'd like you to do a little better.

Professor Quill supervises afternoon study from the chair beside the window. The Institute calls his naps "reflective observation." You are expected to remain at your desk.

> If someone passes you a borrowed key and a note, take your pouch. An urgent errand beats another worksheet. Try to return before Quill wakes.

### Complete your admission record

Choose a **specialty school of magic** from [[Players/02 - Your Student#The Summer Curriculum|the summer curriculum]]. This is your strongest subject.

Record **one background knack** and a connection that still needs your attention: "I know my way around the kitchens, and the cook needs her stolen recipe book back." Your knack means you can handle familiar, safe work without rolling. It doesn't add dice. Your people, promises, and unfinished problems may follow you into an adventure.

Begin at **Level 1 with 10 Composure**. Collect **twelve colored D6s**: four of your school's favored color, four of a different color you choose, and four drawn blindly from the shared component pouch. Your finished twelve-die pouch may contain no more than six dice of any color. Set aside any die that would exceed that limit and draw again. Return the set-aside dice to the shared pouch when you finish. Keep everything in **one opaque pouch**.

### Know what you are studying

Each die stands for an ingredient supply. The color tells you what kind of mana it contains:

| Color  | Mana family        | A familiar ingredient |
| ------ | ------------------ | --------------------- |
| Red    | Fire / Destruction | Brimstone             |
| Blue   | Water / Binding    | Vial of rainwater    |
| Green  | Life / Growth      | Mugwort extract       |
| Brown  | Earth / Shaping    | Clay                  |
| White  | Air / Warding      | Grain of salt          |
| Purple | Mind / Illusion    | Dried mandrake root   |
| Black  | Death / Spirit     | Rowan-wood flakes     |

**The school you use determines how many dice you draw. The mana color determines the form the spell takes.** Choose the school that fits your technique. If it is your specialty, use your specialty draw; otherwise, draw five dice. Choose your intended mana color separately. An evocator may strike with flame, water, roots, or flying books. Consult [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#The Component Cupboard|The Component Cupboard]]. Please use dice at the table; actual brimstone is not required.

### Before attempting anything impressive

Declare your **intended effect, target, school, and mana color**. Agree on the difficulty with the **Professor** before drawing. The **Professor** is the player running the game.

Draw blindly and roll **five dice**, or **seven at Level 1 when the technique belongs to your specialty**. Use your full allowance.

| Exercise    | Required matching faces | Typical undertaking                                           |
| ----------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Routine     | Any pair                | One nearby target or a small obstacle                         |
| Demanding   | Three alike             | A substantial obstacle, a room, or up to three targets        |
| Exceptional | Four alike              | A major magical obstacle, a large area, or up to five targets |

Look at your **largest matching group**. Two 1s are just as good as two 6s; two separate pairs do not make a triple. Meet the requirement and the magic takes hold. Fall short and it **backfires**. Your intention and difficulty remain fixed after the draw.

### Inspect what you actually used

Count **every drawn color**, including dice outside the matching group. A color leads if you drew more dice of that color than of any other. It does not need to make up more than half the draw.

- **The faces succeed and your intended color leads alone:** your intended spell works as you expected.
- **The faces succeed and another color leads alone:** a useful spell works through that mana instead. Fire Sword may become **Fires Word**, pelting the target with romance novels.
- **The faces succeed and colors tie for the lead:** **Wild magic** supplies an unintended useful effect and a complication. A Red/Green Fire Sword might become a flaming rosebush that menaces your pursuer while blocking your route.
- **The faces fall short:** your cast backfires. The leading color shapes the mishap. If colors tie for the lead, combine them into one backfire.

**You normally choose your unexpected effect or backfire.** For successful Wild magic, choose both the useful effect and its complication. Use the drawn colors and stay within the limits for the spell or backfire. If you need help, classmates suggest ideas and the Professor chooses one. If something already in the scene offers an obvious way for the spell to go wrong and still help, the Professor may use it.

If the cast succeeds, you get something useful. Wild magic brings trouble as well, but it still helps. A different color winning doesn't automatically add a penalty. A failed cast remains a backfire however clever the explanation. See [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Approved Casting Procedure|Approved Casting Procedure]].

**Return every die and mix the pouch after every cast.** You used a pinch of the ingredient. Success and failure both leave the supply available.

### How much magic is enough?

Successful spells have **2 points of strength at Routine difficulty, 4 at Demanding, and 6 at Exceptional**. An attack deals that much damage, healing restores that much Composure, and a shield absorbs that much damage. If the largest matching group exceeds the requirement, add **one point**, once. Never add the rolled faces together.

Choose one main effect per cast. Divide points between multiple targets before drawing; four points are four altogether, not four each. Utility magic follows the agreed scope. Duration and particular applications appear in [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps|Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps]].

For backfires, **one die short** means a brief complication or **1 damage**; **two short**, a scene-lasting complication or **2 damage**; **three short**, a major scene problem or **3 damage**. Choose one consequence: damage or a complication. Do not automatically apply both. Missing eyebrows may accompany either at no additional charge.

### When everyone starts shouting

Describe where you are: beside the valve, behind the hedge, or across the greenhouse. Say what you can reach and what stands in your way; you do not need measured distances.

When orderly turns are needed, agree who acts first. Each student gets **one meaningful action**, ordinary movement, and brief speech. After everyone acts, the scene's announced trouble takes its action; a major finale may have two announced actions. The Professor describes evident danger before your turns so you can respond.

Spend your action helping a named classmate with a particular task and their next relevant draw gains **one extra die from their own pouch**. Helpers do not roll separately, bonuses do not stack, and help expires at the end of the next student phase.

Use the same casting rules when you try to sneak, climb, persuade someone, or investigate something risky. Explain your quiet-foot charm, summoned handhold, or other technique. Even avoiding deliberate magic may bring out a reflex enchantment. Safe ordinary actions need no draw: you may walk through an open door without enchanting it.

### Your eyebrows and your whereabouts

Damage reduces your **10 Composure**. At zero you are **Dazed**; classmates have a chance to help before Nurse Bell's wards collect you. Collection brings recovery and inconvenient questions, not removal from the adventure. A safe breather after a pressured scene restores **2 Composure once**; proper rest restores all ten. Consult [[Players/04 - Trouble and Recovery#Conduct During an Incident|Conduct During an Incident]].

Your cohort shares **Faculty Suspicion, from 0 to 6**. Clear evidence raises it: a loud disturbance, an infirmary collection, or a conspicuous mess in a monitored area. A backfire does not raise it automatically. Staff grow curious, investigate, and eventually intervene. Being caught earns a fitting consequence, not the loss of your level or permanent supplies. See [[Professor (DM)/01 - Running the Game#Troublesome Specimens and Faculty Attention|Faculty Attention]].

> [!quote] Advice from an older student
> "We were helping" is a good start. It's rarely a good excuse.

### Specimens and corrective supplies

Helping, exploring, and solving problems can earn a usable ingredient: one die per significant scene, up to three per student per expedition. The Professor names its color before you accept it. Being caught may also bring two compulsory random unwanted dice, up to four corrective dice per expedition. Record additions, mix them into your one pouch, and keep the half-pouch limit. Your draw stays the same. Return these temporary additions at debrief; your normal advancement rewards remain. See [[Players/02 - Your Student#Returning supplies at debrief|Returning supplies at debrief]].

### What you bring home

Afternoon study includes compulsory unfamiliar supplies: **six extra dice at Level 1**, mixed into your existing pouch for eighteen altogether. Take the packet when you slip out. Older students receive larger packets; unwanted ingredients are character-building.

Four major adventures can advance your cohort through **five levels**. At each advancement debrief, return the loaned packet and gain **two permanent dice in colors you choose**, respecting the half-pouch limit. Your specialty draw increases by one die each level: seven at Level 1, eight at Level 2, nine at Level 3, ten at Level 4, and eleven at Level 5. Your ordinary draw stays five. At Level 5, return the whole packet; further debriefs may permit recoloring existing supplies instead of adding more.

Unauthorized expeditions can earn practical credit once you explain what you learned. You still need a hall pass to leave class. You become better prepared, but Prisms keeps finding unfamiliar ingredients for your pouch.

Begin with [[Players/05 - Please Water the Greenhouse#Assignment One: Please Water the Greenhouse|Please Water the Greenhouse]]. Read the student instructions; the sealed appendix belongs to the Professor. There is a note under your worksheet, a borrowed key, and a noise Quill apparently cannot hear.

## About the Institute

**Pengorgon's Remedial Institute for Summer Magical Studies**

**Prisms** in conversation. Use the full name on official correspondence, particularly correspondence concerning repair costs.


> [!quote] Official motto
> A brighter future through adequate supervision.

We provide summer instruction in magical theory, component handling, and judgment. You will catch up with your classmates and demonstrate that last term was an isolated occurrence. If you save the school, please be prepared to explain what endangered it.

Our name reflects the many colors of mana brought together under one roof. Please help keep that roof intact.

### Supervised independent study

Professor Quill oversees your afternoon study period from a comfortable chair. His spectacles remain pointed at the class even when his eyes do not. He calls this "reflective observation." Students call it the best opportunity to leave.

A whispered request, a strange noise, or a promise to a friend can be enough to get you out of your seat. Slip out, investigate, and try to return before Quill finishes resting his eyes. Corridors, prefects, borrowed keys, and evidence of your absence are part of the trouble.

The Institute may give you credit for what you learned while sneaking out. It may also give you detention for sneaking out. Staff will keep you safe, though you may end up finishing your adventure under supervision.
