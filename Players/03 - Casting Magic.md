## The Component Cupboard


> [!quote] Component Handling, First Lesson
> "Label your ingredients before putting them in the pouch. Labeling the pouch 'ingredients' does not count."

### Your component counters

Use a colored six-sided die for each ingredient supply. A spell uses only a pinch, so you keep the supply afterward. The color tells you its **mana type**; matching faces tell you whether the spell works.

Mana describes what magic does. A school of magic, such as evocation, describes how a student casts it. Mind mana can therefore power an evocation spell, as in the Fires Word example in [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Approved Casting Procedure|Spellcasting]].

The cupboard contains seven mana colors. Each covers a family of related effects. Fire and Destruction, for example, share the same Red dice. All counters are ordinary D6s with equal odds. Ingredients in the same mana family follow the same rules. Mandrake and bottled whispers may smell different, but both supply Purple mana.

| Die color | Mana type              | Standard ingredient                                    | Other ingredient equivalents             | Magical effects                                                   |
| --------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Red       | **Fire / Destruction** | Pinch of brimstone (sulfur)                            | Hearth charcoal, candle soot             | Flame, heat, explosive energy, breaking things apart              |
| Blue      | **Water / Binding**    | Saltwater pearl (a dried enchanted droplet), rainwater | Bent pin, knotted thread                 | Water, ice, currents, restraint, pulling linked objects           |
| Green     | **Life / Growth**      | Dried mugwort leaf                                     | Chamomile head, apple seed               | Healing, plants, living transformations, vigorous growth          |
| Brown     | **Earth / Shaping**    | Clay pellet                                            | Pebble, iron-nail clipping               | Stone, soil, metal, weight, reshaping nonliving material          |
| White     | **Air / Warding**      | Grain of salt                                          | Feather tuft, chalk chip, eggshell flake | Wind, sound, clear light, shields, boundaries, cleansing          |
| Purple    | **Mind / Illusion**    | Mandrake-root shaving                                  | Dream ink, bottled whisper               | Thoughts, perception, memory, sleep, words made tangible          |
| Black     | **Death / Spirit**     | Rowan-wood splinter                                    | Grave dust, keepsake scrap               | Decay, ghosts, lingering memories, hauntings, temporary animation |

The die color is a classroom category, not necessarily the ingredient's natural color. Brimstone is not red, and rowan wood is not black. Use the color on the cupboard label.
### Handling notes by mana family

#### Fire / Destruction — Red

Fire makes things brighter, hotter, and considerably more urgent. It releases energy and breaks things apart by burning, bursting, or blasting. A blazing flare belongs to Red; steady revealing light belongs to White.

- Intended spell: a flaming sword.
- Unexpected expression: a shield that protects by becoming too hot to approach.
- Classroom mishap: everything inside the caster's pockets lights up instead of the intended lamp.

#### Mind / Illusion — Purple

Mind acts on perception, thought, dreams, and language. At Prisms, ideas occasionally acquire enough substance to hit someone.

- Intended spell: hide a student behind an illusion.
- Unexpected expression: Fire Sword becomes **Fires Word**, launching romance novels at the target.
- Classroom mishap: an attempt to read thoughts broadcasts the caster's own thoughts to the room.

#### Life / Growth — Green

Growth repairs and reshapes living things. It is excellent at making more life and less reliable at deciding how much is enough.

- Intended spell: heal a scraped knee.
- Unexpected expression: a barrier becomes a rapidly growing hedge.
- Classroom mishap: the student's socks grow roots while the knee is still scraped.

#### Air / Warding — White

White governs air, sound, clear light, and protective boundaries. Wind carries voices, cushions falls, and turns aside flying objects. Warding extends this into removing unwanted magical influences. It can shelter a person, seal a doorway, or contain a troublesome spell.

- Intended spell: protect a classmate from falling debris.
- Unexpected expression: an attack traps its target inside a small containment bubble.
- Classroom mishap: the ward refuses entry to everyone, including its caster.

#### Water / Binding — Blue

Blue governs water, ice, and connections. It flows, freezes, joins, restrains, and pulls. A current carries things physically; a binding moves them through a magical connection. Free movement through the air belongs to White.

- Intended spell: pull a dropped key back into the caster's hand.
- Unexpected expression: an attack ties the target's shoelaces together.
- Classroom mishap: the key stays put and the student is pulled toward it.

#### Death / Spirit — Black

Black governs decay, lingering presences, ghosts, and the impressions things leave behind. Decay ages or weakens material; Red destroys it through released force. It can also let an object briefly behave as though someone is home inside it. You will study it alongside the other subjects. Working with spirits does not make you evil.

- Intended spell: ask a portrait what happened in the corridor.
- Unexpected expression: an attack is delivered by the ghost of an opinionated fencing instructor.
- Classroom mishap: the desk answers the question and begins reporting every incident of cheating it has witnessed.

#### Earth / Shaping — Brown

Brown governs soil, stone, metal, and the shape of nonliving things. It builds, reinforces, changes weight, and persuades solid material to take a different form.

- Intended spell: raise a stone step to reach a window.
- Unexpected expression: a sword spell produces an enormous floppy clay blade.
- Classroom mishap: the classroom floor buckles instead of forming the requested step.

### Selecting your intended mana

Declare the color that describes **how your spell works**, rather than its purpose alone. A shield might be a White ward, a Blue wall of ice, a Green hedge, or a Brown stone barrier. Healing living tissue belongs to Green; consulting a departed healer belongs to Black.

A spell can use one part of a color's family without requiring the other. A water spell does not have to bind anything. You don't need another skill or roll to use a different part of the family.

If more than one color could do the job, choose how you want to do it. That doesn't grant extra dice or require several colors. Each spell declares one intended color, and the draw determines its actual expression.

### Cupboard regulations

1. **One die represents one ingredient supply. Each cast uses only a pinch.** A purple die might represent mandrake or a bottled whisper; either contributes one Mind die when drawn.
2. **Keep a supply record.** Record the number of counters of each mana type. Your pouch must match that record before each practical.
3. **Draw without looking or feeling for a particular die.** Use dice of the same size and shape in an opaque pouch.
4. **Ingredients of the same mana type work identically.** A rare specimen may matter to an assignment, but rarity does not grant an extra modifier.
5. **Return all dice after each cast.** Mix the pouch before the next draw. Ordinary casting does not consume dice or reduce ingredient supplies.

### Library references

The library recommends these sources for comparison with historical ingredient lore. Our seven-color curriculum is not a universal historical system. Several sources concern protection against witchcraft and its supposed effects.

- **Brimstone and salt:** the ancient Mesopotamian anti-witchcraft series *Maqlû* addresses sulfur and salt in incantations intended to release a person from sorcery. Fire and Ward are our classroom assignments, not categories established by that source. See [SBL Press: The Witchcraft Series Maqlû, introductory material](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/archive/pubs/061537P-front.pdf).
- **Mandrake:** medieval herbal lore gave mandrake elaborate magical harvesting stories. The Mind classification belongs to this curriculum; the source records harvesting lore, not a medieval category of mental mana. See [British Library: How to harvest a mandrake](https://www.bl.uk/stories/blogs/posts/how-to-harvest-a-mandrake).
- **Healing herbs:** the *Lacnunga* contains the Old English Nine Herbs Charm, combining herbs and spoken charms. It inspires Growth's mixture of healing and botanical magic. See [British Library: Harley MS 585](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-001949807) and [Nine Herbs Charm translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation%3ANine_Herbs_Charm).
- **Pins and sympathetic magic:** a museum witch bottle contains hair and pins, while a documented pin-stuck onion was used in sympathetic magic. Our Binding lessons adapt the idea of a connection between an object and a subject; these records document the objects and their uses. See [Museum of Witchcraft and Magic: Witch bottle 3938](https://museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk/object/witch-bottle-6/) and [Pitt Rivers Museum: Tylor's Onion](https://web.prm.ox.ac.uk/england/englishness-tylors-onion.html).
- **Rowan:** surviving rowan charms were intended to protect against witches, faeries, and ghosts. Our Spirit curriculum expands the protective association; this historical record does not establish ghost-summoning use. See [Pitt Rivers Museum: Rowan loop](https://web.prm.ox.ac.uk/amulets/index.php/museum-trail/15-resources/146-trail-rowan-loop/index.html).

Saltwater pearls, dream ink, bottled whispers, paired mana families, and the effects taught above belong to our curriculum, rather than these historical records. Other equivalents are classroom conventions, not additional historical claims.

## Approved Casting Procedure


> [!quote] Practical Examination Instructions
> Successfully casting a spell and casting the requested spell are marked separately.

### Before opening your pouch

Tell the Professor what you want the spell to do, who or what it affects, and which **school and mana color** you plan to use. Agree on the difficulty before you draw. The technique has to fit the school: calling a falling brick "an exercise in Enchantment" won't get you a bigger handful.

Once you draw, stick with the intention and difficulty you agreed on.

### The seven steps

1. **Declare your intention.** Say what you want to do, which school you are using, and which mana color you intend.
2. **Confirm difficulty.** Routine requires two alike, Demanding three, Exceptional four.
3. **Draw blindly.** Draw five dice, or your specialty allowance for your level. Add one die if a classmate is helping. Keep each die out until the cast is over. If you need more dice than the pouch holds, draw the whole pouch once.
4. **Roll and inspect the largest matching group.** Any face value qualifies. Meet or exceed the required difficulty to succeed.
5. **Count every drawn color.** Count the dice that matched and those that didn't. The most common color shapes the spell. A tie for most common means Wild magic.
6. **Resolve the result.** Consult the outcome table below and [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps|Spell Effects and Backfires]].
7. **Return every die and mix the pouch.** Each cast uses only a pinch of the ingredients. Keep your supplies whether the spell works or backfires.

Take your full allowance without looking or feeling for particular dice. Keep what you draw and roll it once. You can only change the draw when a rule says so; a harder spell doesn't earn extra dice.

### What constitutes a match

| Difficulty | Required group | Qualifying example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | At least 2 alike | Two 1s, two 6s, or any other pair |
| Demanding | At least 3 alike | Three 2s or any other triple |
| Exceptional | At least 4 alike | Four 5s or any other group of four |

A pair of 1s is as good as a pair of 6s. **Two pairs do not make a triple.** A larger group also passes an easier difficulty and may earn the strength bonus in [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps|Spell Effects and Backfires]].

A smaller group produces a backfire even if it would have passed an easier exercise.

> [!quote] Student annotation
> Seven dice always contain a pair. The trick is persuading the mana to be the spell you wanted.

### Recording the outcome

| Numerical result | Color result                        | Outcome                                                                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meets difficulty | Intended color leads alone          | Your intended spell succeeds.                                          |
| Meets difficulty | Another color leads alone           | Your spell succeeds through an unexpected expression of that mana.     |
| Meets difficulty | Two or more colors tie for the lead | Wild magic: an unintended spell takes hold.                            |
| Below difficulty | Any color result                    | Your cast backfires; its colors supply the mishap's style and outcome. |

The leading color just needs more dice than each other color. It doesn't need more than half the handful. Three Red, two Blue, and two Green resolve as Red. Two Red, two Blue, and one Green are Wild. A tie below the leader does not count.

#### Unanticipated substitution

When a different color wins, try to find another way for the spell to do the job. Purple may turn Fire Sword into **Fires Word**. A Green shield may become a hedge; a Blue shield may become ice. If the new mana can't do what you wanted, the successful cast still gives you something useful.

#### Wild results

When colors tie, use them together to imagine what happens. You can't pick your favorite and count it as the intended spell. There is no Wild ingredient to buy.

**The caster chooses what the Wild spell does and what trouble comes with it.** Use the tied colors and what's happening around you. It doesn't have to do the job you planned, but the Professor checks that it stays within the agreed scope and strength.

If everyone is stuck, let the spell do the job in an unexpected way and add one complication from the tied colors. Successful Wild complications do not automatically damage the caster. A failed Wild cast receives **one normal backfire**, not an additional successful spell or one backfire per color.

A Red/Green tie on Fire Sword might produce a flaming rosebush: useful against pursuers, inconvenient for your intended route, and difficult to explain to Groundskeeper Moss.

### Explaining an unintended result

You normally choose how your own spell goes sideways, whether it's a useful substitution, Wild magic, or a backfire. Use the leading mana and stay within the result's scope, strength, or severity. You don't have to come up with a pun.

Where the scene already offers an obvious way to succeed by going wrong, the Professor may follow that opening directly. A misplaced gust might knock the sought-after key off its hook and into the soup. That twist has to come from something already in the scene, and a successful roll still gives you a useful result. If the faces fell short, it's a backfire, even if the accident happens to help.

**Ask your classmates if you get stuck.** They can suggest ideas until the Professor chooses one. The Professor also checks your own suggestion against the rules; work out any changes together. There's no extra roll. Use the incident ledger for ideas when you need it.

A wrong-color success gives you something useful without an automatic penalty. A successful Wild spell needs both a useful benefit and a concrete complication. A backfire needs the consequence specified by its shortfall. Keep the result you rolled as you describe what happened.
### An example from the marking ledger

A Level 1 Evocation specialist attempts a Routine Fire Sword and draws seven dice.

- **Faces:** 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6. A pair meets Routine difficulty. The two pairs do not form a triple.
- **Colors:** one Red, six Purple. Purple leads alone.
- **Result:** Fires Word: the target is pelted with romance novels.

The same faces fail a Demanding attempt, falling short by one. A Minor Purple backfire might send a book flying back at the student.

### Keeping a useful allowance

What you put in the pouch affects what comes out. More of a color makes it more likely to lead; a spread of colors brings more variety and more chances of a tie. New supplies can make your favorite color less common, even though the pouch is fuller.

Observe the Balanced Reagent Policy: no color exceeds half the pouch. Mix the six-to-fourteen compulsory practical dice into that **same pouch**. Record the loaned colors on paper and draw everything from the same pouch. See [[Players/02 - Your Student#Admissions and Progress|Students and Advancement]].

## Permitted Effects and Common Mishaps


> [!quote] Practical Magic, Chapter One
> Decide what the spell should do before discovering what it has done.

This chapter tells you how much a spell can do and what happens when it goes wrong. If someone ends up on the floor, turn to [[Players/04 - Trouble and Recovery#Conduct During an Incident|Conduct During an Incident]].

### Declare the exercise

Before drawing, declare your intended **effect**, **target**, **school**, and **mana color**. The Professor sets the difficulty from what the spell needs to do. Decide what it does before naming it.

Choose one main effect for each cast. An attack can sparkle as much as you like, but healing, binding, and opening a portal are separate effects. Healing, attacking, and opening a portal each need their own action and cast. A higher difficulty lets the chosen effect do more.

### Casting scope

| Difficulty  | Required match | Typical scope                                                              | Effect strength |
| ----------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Routine     | 2 alike        | One nearby target, a small object, or one immediate obstacle               | 2 points        |
| Demanding   | 3 alike        | Up to three nearby targets, a room-sized effect, or a substantial obstacle | 4 points total  |
| Exceptional | 4 alike        | Up to five nearby targets, a large area, or a major magical obstacle       | 6 points total  |

Even Exceptional spells have limits. Unlimited wishes and permanent resurrection need special circumstances agreed with your Professor.

**Nearby** means within the same immediate scene and clearly reachable by the described magic. A spell that moves one person between nearby positions is usually Routine; crossing a substantial gap or moving a group is Demanding. Ordinary safe movement needs no cast. Judge reach from the actual obstacle; no measured range rules are needed.

Use whichever is harder: the spell's size or the work it needs to do. A small, heavily enchanted lock can be harder than a large mundane one. You may narrow your intention before rolling to qualify for an easier exercise.

### Marking strength

A successful spell has the strength listed for its difficulty. Rolled face values determine matches; they are not added together for damage or healing.

If the **largest matching group exceeds the requirement**, add **one point of strength**, once per cast. A Routine spell with three or more alike therefore has strength 3. Separate pairs do not add strength. The bonus does not grant extra targets, duration, or another main effect.

If the spell doesn't use points, an extra-strong match makes it cleaner or more precise. It still does the job you agreed on; it doesn't solve another problem for free.

### Practical applications

#### Attack

Deal the spell's strength in damage. For multiple targets, divide the total strength between them before drawing, assigning at least one point to each. A Demanding attack dealing 4 points would assign 2 to each of two targets, rather than dealing 4 to everyone.

If an oversized match grants the one-point bonus, assign it to one of those already declared targets after rolling. The same rule applies to divided shields and healing. Do not select a new target after seeing the result.

A different mana color can change how the attack looks while keeping its damage. Flames, flying books, icy pellets, or grasping roots can all deal damage.

#### Protection

Create a shield that absorbs damage up to its strength, then ends. It lasts until exhausted or the immediate scene ends. For several protected targets, divide the strength before drawing as with an attack.

Use only the strongest remaining shield on each student; shields do not stack. Damage beyond its remaining protection reduces Composure. See [[Players/04 - Trouble and Recovery#Protective arrangements|Protective arrangements]].

Protection against a specific non-damaging hazard, such as smoke or a minor haunting, uses the same difficulty benchmarks instead of an absorption total. It protects against that declared hazard, not every possible consequence.

#### Healing

Restore Composure equal to strength, up to the target's maximum. Divide healing between multiple targets before drawing. Green restores a battered student's composure and tends their scrapes; a wrong-color cast provides an unexpected benefit of comparable scale rather than automatically restoring Composure with every color.

For example, a White result might supply a protective air cushion around an injured student; Purple might hide them from a pursuing threat. Those substitutions help the student, but don't restore their lost Composure. Restoring Composure to a Dazed student cancels infirmary collection as described in [[Players/04 - Trouble and Recovery#Conduct During an Incident|Turns Trouble and Recovery]]; concealing their distress alone does not.

#### Movement and utility

Move, reveal, conceal, reshape, or otherwise resolve the declared obstacle at the agreed scope. Most utility effects do not need point totals.

- Routine: lift a key, illuminate a classroom, conceal one student, grow a foothold, or ask a cooperative ghost one question.
- Demanding: move three students across a gap, conceal a small group, reshape a heavy door, or uncover information through magical interference.
- Exceptional: move five students past a major barrier, reshape a large structure, or contain a major magical disturbance long enough to act.

Treat these as one immediate action or one scene of effect. Longer-lasting changes need an agreed limit before rolling. Asking the same divination question again won't turn up new information unless something has changed.

#### Duration, restraint, and influence

Agree on duration before drawing. A scene means the current continuous situation with the same immediate stakes; moving into the next room does not automatically end pursuit, damage, or a troublesome enchantment. You can't claim a fresh scene just to recover again or get rid of a consequence.

- **Momentary distraction:** prevents one announced threat action when the description explains how. It ends after that threat phase. Repeating it requires another action and cast, and an intelligent threat can change tactics.
- **Ongoing restraint or concealment:** lasts through the scene, but its scope must fit the target. Restraining a nuisance can be Routine, a scene threat Demanding, and a major problem Exceptional. Threats can spend their announced action breaking an ordinary binding or searching; an effect strong enough to contain the whole threat follows its stated solution difficulty.
- **Influence:** wins one plausible concession at Routine scope. Compelling serious cooperation or changing a deeply held position is harder and must fit the scene; a Routine charm cannot resolve an entire quest by ordering everyone to obey.
- **Physical changes:** opened doors, moved objects, and repaired channels remain changed. Temporary enchantments expire at their agreed end. Ordinary environmental consequences require attention rather than vanishing solely because casting has stopped.

You may dismiss your own ongoing enchantment on your turn without another roll or meaningful action, provided the effect remains under your control. Dismissal does not reverse damage, refund Composure, or undo a runaway Wild effect or backfire. Such trouble needs a suitable solution.

You can invent spells of your own using these limits. Tell the Professor what you want to do and agree on the effect before drawing.

### When the ingredients disagree

The faces tell you whether the spell takes hold; its difficulty sets the strength. The colors tell you what form it takes. Follow [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Approved Casting Procedure|Spellcasting]]. A wrong-color result should retain the original purpose where possible; it may substitute a useful benefit when the new mana cannot reasonably accomplish that purpose.

A successful **Wild** result produces an unintended spell from the tied leading colors. Its scope may not exceed the agreed difficulty; its total points remain the recorded spell strength. The caster chooses the useful effect and complication under [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Explaining an unintended result|Explaining an unintended result]]. The spell still succeeds, even if you have some explaining to do afterward.

Example: a Routine Red/Green tie on Fire Sword creates a flaming rosebush. It can threaten the target with 2 damage, but then obstructs the caster's route. It does not deal full damage every turn indefinitely.

### Incident grading

If the largest matching group falls short of difficulty, the intended spell fails and a backfire occurs. Use the leading mana color to theme it; a leading-color tie produces a Wild backfire combining those colors.

Compare the required group with the largest rolled group. If no faces match, that largest group is one.

| Shortfall | Severity | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 die short | Minor | One brief complication or 1 damage to the caster |
| 2 dice short | Serious | One scene-lasting complication or 2 damage to the caster |
| 3 dice short | Spectacular | A major scene problem or 3 damage to the caster |

Choose a complication **or** damage for the backfire; do not automatically apply both. Soot and missing eyebrows may accompany either without extra penalties. Even a serious mishap leaves the student and their classmates something to do.

A backfire does not also deal successful attack damage to the intended target. Ingredients still return to the pouch. Matching faces below difficulty do not turn a failed spell into a successful one.

### Reported incidents by mana

Choose or invent your own mishap matching the leading mana and recorded severity, following [[Players/03 - Casting Magic#Explaining an unintended result|Explaining an unintended result]]. If you need help, classmates suggest incidents and the Professor chooses one. The ledger below offers examples; it requires no additional roll.

| Mana | Minor | Serious | Spectacular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red — Fire / Destruction | A sleeve smolders; patting it out occupies the next action. | The pouch emits smoke, making concealment difficult for the scene. | A burst of heat starts a spreading fire in the immediate area that needs attention. |
| Blue — Water / Binding | The caster's shoelaces knot together until untied. | The caster is tethered to a nearby object until the binding is broken. | A local flood carries loose objects and blocks the planned route. |
| Green — Life / Growth | Flowers sprout over the caster's eyes until brushed aside. | Clothing roots to the floor and must be freed. | Aggressive vegetation divides the room and traps important objects or people. |
| Brown — Earth / Shaping | A held tool becomes floppy clay until reshaped. | The caster's boots become heavy stone for the scene unless repaired. | Part of the floor buckles, creating a barrier and unstable footing. |
| White — Air / Warding | A gust scatters the caster's loose notes. | A ward seals the wrong doorway until removed. | A containment bubble encloses the cohort with the problem they were trying to exclude. |
| Purple — Mind / Illusion | The caster speaks their next thought aloud. | An illusion conceals the real exit for the scene until identified. | A shared hallucination creates a convincing immediate hazard; investigating it reveals the deception. |
| Black — Death / Spirit | A nearby object begins loudly criticizing the caster. | A ghost follows the cohort and interferes with their work until appeased. | Several objects acquire unruly presences and begin rearranging the scene. |

Staff attention can follow a visible or noisy mishap when it makes sense in the scene. Use the shared Faculty Suspicion track in [[Professor (DM)/01 - Running the Game#Troublesome Specimens and Faculty Attention|Threats and Faculty]]; do not add automatic Suspicion for every backfire.

### Repeating an exercise

You can try a spell again because the ingredients go back in your pouch. During a pressured scene, each attempt should consume an action or meaningful opportunity; a failed attempt changes the situation through its backfire. A later attempt is a new cast, not a free retry.

If there's no pressure and nothing much could go wrong, the Professor can let a Routine spell work as intended. Draw and roll when the result or its surprises would matter. During safe downtime, healing can restore Composure up to its maximum. See [[Players/04 - Trouble and Recovery#Conduct During an Incident|Turns Trouble and Recovery]] for rest and recovery.

An automatic success does not earn the extra strength for a larger match. For magical protection or other preparations before danger, establish the situation first: if the mana result could matter, make the cast. Time spent repeating attempts gives an active threat or approaching deadline an opportunity to advance. Do not freely stockpile shields; the non-stacking rule applies throughout the continuous scene.
